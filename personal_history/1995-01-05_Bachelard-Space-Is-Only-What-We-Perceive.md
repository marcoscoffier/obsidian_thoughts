# Bachelard: Space Is Only What We Perceive

1/5/95 ~~Wordsworth and the worth of words~~
Bachelard never explores a space that does not take on the characteristics he has described for a home. The universe comes to protect once it is intimate, not the 'other' it is home-like
Space is relative like time is relative.
Space changes w/ changes in the percievers's condition
\- 15 minutes in the ~~arms~~ lap of one's lover is far shorter than 15 spent sitting on a burner
~~[?]~~ The same space can ~~change a~~ be percieved of as being as radically different as those two spans of time.
Time does seem to be more akin to the art of poetry or literature - a narrative art. But ~~[?]~~ a single poem or poetic image can be quite easily compared to a painting. In the work of Merleau Ponty and perhaps in the poetry of the 20th c. we have a time when poetry does describe these perceptual moments and since ~~space~~ our concept of space is formed by these perceptions, poetry can describe <u>space</u> - psycological or phenomenological space.
↳ I'm not in a position to make this differentiation yet.
`[a bracket in the margin joins this paragraph to the last line:]`
Phenomenology or Merleau Ponty seems to take an artistic stand on theory or maybe even Philosophy - if you feel it, it is there - it is! Truth is in the moment. He begins the "Avant-Propos" of <u>Phenoménologie de la perception</u> w/:
Qu'est-ce que la phenomenologie? Il peut paraître étrange qu'on ait encore à poser cette question un demi-siècle après les premiers travaux de Husserl. Elle est pourtant loin d'être résolue. La phenomenolgie, c'est l'étude des essences, et tout les problèmes, selon elle, reviennent à definir des essences: l'essence de la perception, l'essence de la conscience, par example
nous ne trouvons dans les textes que ce que nous y avons mis...
\-hmm...
→ Space is only what we percieve it to be.

> [!note] Transcription notes
> The heading "Wordsworth and the worth of words" is crossed out. Bachelard's book on the home and intimate space is *La Poétique de l'espace* (1957). The French passage is copied from the opening of Merleau-Ponty's *Phénoménologie de la perception* (1945).
