# Boring Lecture, and Sky Color

During the painfully boring lecture
~~That~~ 2 milk cartons were backlit ~~[?]~~ on ~~the~~ very reflective surface of the table. And Michael Rock kept passing through the light behind them blending his shadow and reflection with theirs. His dark (black) pants made a blue flash in the gap between the two lamp's shadows.
`[in pencil:]` The a
1st week - set up a piece I called Sky Color.
`[in blue pen, with a curved arrow pointing into the pencil paragraph below:]` → the <u>potential</u> of this work is what I am so excited about is what gives it anything beyond transcendent!
`[in pencil:]` I felt today that there was something careful & deliberate in the way I had set this up. It made me happy to ~~think~~ notice how.
`[in blue pen:]` I cannot ~~set~~ set something up so that you definitely see something - I can put out receptors captors, frames but beyond that, ~~I am out~~ it is out of my control. It could be that you feel nothing that I wanted you to feel, but the most exciting is that ~~I can~~ the work can become something I never expected or thought of - that is where it is most powerful. It opens you up to wonder and recurring newness of nature itself and life - if a manic depressive were made aware of this newness this replenishing power he would no longer have a problem.
`[doodles in the lower right corner, in black and red: a stick figure at a lectern, "BORING!", "help!", "BORING 1 hr. down, 1 hr. to go", and a game of tic-tac-toe in a circle]`

> [!note] Transcription notes
> Undated. Three inks: the lecture observation in black, two pencil lines about "Sky Color", and the blue-pen paragraphs, which were probably added later around the pencil. Michael Rock is a graphic designer who taught at the Yale School of Art; he may have been the lecturer. The doodles are from the same boring lecture.
