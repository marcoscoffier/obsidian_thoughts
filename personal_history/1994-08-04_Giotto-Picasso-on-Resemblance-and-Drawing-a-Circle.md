# Giotto, The Ambassadors & Picasso on Drawing a Perfect Circle

Observations from Boccaccio, perspective viewing angles, and Arnheim (**pp. 137–138**, leading directly into page 104):

- `[Boccacio's Decameron(e) speaks of Giotto - highly stylized = super reality -> like Zeuxis story of grapes in Pliny.]`
- Reflects on how a 2D painting hanging on a wall among 3D objects depicts 3D space yet remains flat—even viewed from sharp angles, the rectangle warps (`[4 trapezoid/parallelogram sketches]`) without forming composite spatial depth: `Take photographs of paintings from sharp angles to get warped images — like "Ambassadors"`.
- **Arnheim, p. 137 (cultural style & illusion):** Summarizes Arnheim's note that actual illusions are rare mainly because each culture perceives its own familiar style of representation directly as a faithful reproduction of the object.
- **Picasso (1966 / p. 138):**
  - Notes Picasso's 1966 remark that he aims at resemblance—observing nature while never confusing it with painting, since nature is translatable into painting only *"by signs."*
  - Notes Picasso's advice on striving for perfection: try to draw a perfect circle and the involuntary flaw reveals your personality, whereas trying deliberately to draw an imperfect, personal circle bungles the whole thing (`- p. 138`). *(Leads directly to Arnheim p. 138 on originality vs. personal style on notebook page 104.)*
