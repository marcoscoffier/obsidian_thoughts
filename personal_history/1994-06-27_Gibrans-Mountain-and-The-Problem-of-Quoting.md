# Gibran's Mountain & The Problem of Quoting

From Khalil Gibran (which I quoted from Cara's yearbook page):
> *"When you part from your friend, you grieve not; for that which you love most in him may be clearer in his absence, as the mountain to the climber is from the plain."*

Gibran has reversed what I normally think: the plain is clearer from the mountain. Even the climber must spend more time at his home than on the mountain, and I don't know if the mountain really becomes clearer—it becomes conceivable as a block rather than as a series of images of hand- and footholds, or of paths through changing territory. The view of the city from the plane (not plain) does not add anything to the city-goer's knowledge of the flowers in the shop or the trash in the drain; rather, it creates a map where these events can be put in a relationship.
