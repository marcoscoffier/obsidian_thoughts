Wittgenstein - <u>later</u>.
Now... saw language itself as the spawner of problematic issues, and the exercise of coming to understand how we use language as the therapy for philosophical problems. In his later work Witt. did not try to solve problems but tried rather to show that they arise from a network of terms that have evolved in such a way as to make their disentanglement extremely difficult. As he once commented, his aim in philosophy was <u>to show the fly the way out of the fly-bottle</u>. p. 68
\- language is inherently public or communal.
"it is instructive to think of language as a set of games: we proceed from the fact that we are always involved in many language games - interactions with other individuals in which we move around sets of linguistic counters; and like a set of games each of these little `[circled:]` en counters has its own set of rules. But it is not easy to ferret out these rules b/c they overlap w/ one another; the language games constantly mesh. To add to this tangled state of affairs, words do not have clear, `[above:]` unambiguous meanings, ~~with no definition ever sufficent to account for all and only all, games.~~
