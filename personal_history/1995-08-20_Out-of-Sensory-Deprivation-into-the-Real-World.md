# Out of Sensory Deprivation into the Real World

8/20/95
\- I was reading <u>Art & Tech</u> catalogue I got today and was thinking of bringing a viewer from the anewhatever (sensory deprivation) space into the real world Not into a Ganzfield environment like Irwin & Turrell ~~where~~ were thinking about. I would want to make their process much simpler - take away all perceptions from the viewer so that when they are brought back to the world they just left, it seems brilliant.

**Next page:**

\- I'm thinking of my isolation tower again
\- and still thinking about the audio feedback -
→ sensory
Ganzfield which appears & disappears depending on your body rhythyms - brain wave - alpha - detector - Hewlett/Packard.
I was also thinking of continuing painting & photo but separating their functions -
\- paint <u>effect</u> w/ a sharp point of focus
↳ something looks brighter or darker when it is in the periphery of your vision.
→ photography is a different kind of instantaneous sees w/ its own eye which you can control, but is not like yours. The human eye only focuses on less than 5% of viewing field, ~~[?]~~ which is much wider than photography I like how photography can be just a simple framing of the world - does not put anything there which isn't there to begin with, only presents it to you in the photographer's way.
I wish I had a photo of the rock I was lying next to @ the beach and of Hyon In-Sung & Tina in the light of the gas station with the pale late twilight behind them. I need to look up in Meinel & Meinel what that green stripe, w/ red next to it was up from the spot where the sun set
`[sketch: a horizon with the setting sun and rays rising from it, labelled "green" and "sunset"; to the right, three heads under a tall light pole, labelled "gas station lights on faces"]`

> [!note] Transcription notes
> The "Art & Tech" catalogue is probably *A Report on the Art and Technology Program of the Los Angeles County Museum of Art, 1967–1971*, in which Irwin and Turrell worked with the psychologist Edward Wortz on perception; "anewhatever" is probably a stab at "anechoic" (an anechoic chamber). The isolation tower is the one in [[1995-03-12_Asking-the-Viewer-to-Do-Things-a-Tower]]. "Meinel & Meinel" is Aden and Marjorie Meinel's *Sunsets, Twilights, and Evening Skies* (1983); the green stripe is probably the green flash. Hyon In-Sung and Tina are not identified.
