## [[1994-07-28_Turning-From-Science-to-Art-and-Robinson-Crusoe|Turning From Science to Art, Commuters & Robinson Crusoe (Page 85)]]
*1994-07-28 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 85 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p014.jpg|p. 14]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p015.jpg|p. 15]]*

I don't think of my turn away from the sciences as a defeat. I guess I could. But, I saw that I could get a lot more out of the arts. The arts are more readily accessible to what I want to get out of life.

Every once in a while I look around, feeling the power of art in my veins, or looking at the world like an artist, and I wonder what all these other people are doing. Why do they get dressed up to go to meetings where presumably they talk of important interesting things & then fall into the most trivial gossip on the subway or train. If their work is so fascinating, how can they turn off like that. I never turn off. ##### Summary of Arnheim Excerpts
- **pp. 44–45 (*Detail*):** Summarizes anthropologists' finding that people unfamiliar with photography struggle to read "realistic" photos that Western viewers have learned to decipher, and that overall structural features (such as triangularity) are primary perceptual data rather than late abstractions.
- **p. 62 (*Simplicity — form & meaning in image & language*):** Summarizes Arnheim's argument that every artwork conveys meaning about existence (like a teapot interpreting its function to the eye), so simplicity depends on matching visual/verbal structure to intended thought—short sentences do not guarantee simplicity if form conflicts with meaning.

## [[1994-10-05_Kant-and-Batesons-Double-Bind|Kant & Bateson's Double Bind]]
*1994-10-05 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 10 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p015.jpg|p. 15]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p016.jpg|p. 16]]*

Kant articulated a profound problem: the human mind can have no direct, mirror-like knowledge of the objective world, for the object it experiences has already been structured by the subject's own internal organization. The human being knows not the world-in-itself, but rather the world-as-rendered-by-the-human-mind (much like the mediation of the artist, especially with photography).

Substitute "world" for "mother" and "human being" for "child" in Gregory Bateson's "double bind" (which eventually leads a person to become schizophrenic):
1. The human being's relation to the world is one of vital dependency, making it critical to assess the nature of that world accurately.
2. The human mind receives contradictory or incompatible information about its situation with respect to the world, rendering its inner psychological and spiritual sense of things incoherent with scientific meta-communication.
3. Epistemologically, the human mind cannot achieve direct communication with the world.
4. Existentially, the human being cannot leave the field.
