## [[1994-06-15_Letter-to-Edo-Part-1-Shared-Experience|Letter to Edo (Part 1 — Shared Experience)]]
*1994-06-15 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 18 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p027.jpg|p. 27]]*

1) I'm not talking for Mom, or using Mom's words. Mom & I are very similar, her outlook is in many respects very similar to my own.
- You have no idea how much you are hurting her.
- I hear so much about you secondhand. It is very hard not to have an intimate relationship w/ your own brother. I feel you don't tell me things, for the same reason you don't tell Mom things.
- I understand the feeling that you want to withdraw from society & do your own thing, like those guys in the cabin in Waterford—like [an] acid trip—this is for you & only you; it is not a shared experience. I don't know if you are any good at the guitar at all, I've never heard you play—only mess around.

You are so critical of everyone, I can only imagine how critical you are of yourself. I believe that one's *[crossed out: your]* life doesn't really mean anything unless one has *[crossed out: you have]* shared it with others—the more the better.

- I don't buy this "can't grow under the shade of an oak" shit—the you and Richie lame-out. Whatever I have done, it's no big wup (deal); hundreds of thousands of people have done more, better. The fact is that the world is so absolutely full of fascinating things to learn & experience & then pass on that there can never be enough people to do it. You are only on this earth for so many years. Make the most of it. Take everything that comes your way & embrace it, learn from it, let it change you, don't settle on any revelation—it will get boring. Acid is not some hidden mystery, which will revolutionize the world if people would only try it.

## [[1994-06-15_Letter-to-Edo-Part-2-Yale-and-Individuation|Letter to Edo (Part 2 — Yale & Individuation)]]
*1994-06-15 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 19 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p028.jpg|p. 28]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p029.jpg|p. 29]]*

Question everything, which is so in your nature it sounds silly to tell you this—I have learned from you to question many things I may have taken for granted.

I get slammed every day at Yale—that's how I'm learning. Let yourself get slammed, admit that you have been slammed in front of a huge crowd and fucked up even though you were trying your hardest. Know that you did the best you could, and sucked. It doesn't mean you suck. Find out why people thought you sucked and you learn more, quicker, than any other way. It's very easy to convince yourself, with any group of people that love you or are your friends, that you are great.

Someday I hope that whatever decision you make in your life, you would be able to come back to anyone that thought you were doing the wrong thing and prove that they were wrong—to show them up, whoever they are.

It's not going to happen if you hang around burnouts... Believe me, you are so fucking smart you surprise me. I would count you smarter, more perceptive, and more constantly thinking than 90% of the people at Yale. Why would Yale not accept you? Believe me, it's not that you couldn't handle it.

## [[1994-07-15_The-Chopin-Nocturne-Room|Installation Idea: The Chopin Nocturne Room]]
*1994-07-15 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 65 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p110.jpg|p. 110]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p112.jpg|p. 112]]*

My next idea was to create a piece for the Chopin Nocturne:
* **Entrance:** You enter a room and have to go around a wall upon which I have written a poem about my experience of going to sleep with this music—my mother would play it to calm me down when I was a baby.
* **Architecture:** A ramp leads you down into a dark room with a low ceiling and mattresses or cushions on the ground.
* **Lighting & Sound:** Venetian blinds with daylight-like lights behind them to simulate windows so the light disperses in stripes, lights diffusing gently along the walls, and hidden speakers.
* **Threshold:** A secret passageway—something you could pass right by without noticing, with no door, just a subtle light above a doorway. It doesn't scream *ENTRANCE*, just a place you notice and sneak into to find magic: *"Am I supposed to be coming back here? Is this off-limits?"* Inside, an unobstructed lifeline back out which you choose not to take.

## [[1994-07-27_Hull-Bonding-With-His-Son-Through-Drawing|Hull & His Son Drawing as Equals, Plus Sky Painting Tips (Page 81)]]
*1994-07-27 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 81 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p007.jpg|p. 7]]*

`*` Before coming over Hull went by the comic store to buy his kid some comics that he could read in the car on the way to Baltimore: *"We bond through drawing"* Hull said, *"He loves drawing super-heroes (Thats all I drew when I was young - Hull)"*. He showed me a small sketch book he had taken to the ballgame with his son. *"He could never sit through a whole game, he doesn't understand it, but if we're drawing he'll stay 3 hrs."* In the book the pages alternated Dad & son. *"With drawing we can be equals, I'm not teaching him anything. We're both artists."* A strange comment coming from a Yale professor. I know he's dying to have his son become an artist which was hard for him & will be for me (if I decide to do it) because it was so foreign to what our parents did. It will be absolutely natural for Hull's son — both his parents are artists, And he will have been drawing the way I would listen to music w/ my mom, he's not even 5 yet. Those memories from before real memory begins are so powerful.

Before I forget, Hull had two tips about painting large flat planes: mix 3 big pots up first of maybe 3 sky colors, to have enough to cover whole sky & maybe I should paint those areas w/ shorter strokes in the quick paintings, I pick up my brush, whereas the longer ones are constantly scumbled. I have to find if the high detail multiple reflections is really for me or something more about the gesture — maybe both?

I was thinking about French metaphors (or dead metaphors really) today in the car listening to my dad speak.

## [[1994-07-31_Mortality-Andre-Diard-and-Tignes|Mortality, André Diard & Summers in Tignes]]
*1994-07-31 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · scan [[attachments/Screenshot_2026-11-18_at_10.26.11_AM.png|Screenshot_2026-11-18_at_10.26.11_AM]]*

- This journal and any art possibly can be seen as a fight against death. I want to maximize my time before death—do the most w/ my life & thoughts, & possibly leave something worthwhile behind.

**7/31/94**  
André Diard died this winter. He was a key figure in the town of Tignes and a fixed memory since he ran the rental of our apartment at Les Chaudes Almes. ## [[1994-07-31_Oncle-Henri-Camus-and-Conceptual-Space-in-French|Oncle Henri en Français, le Père de Camus & les Cartes Mentales (Page 94)]]

Je pense que je devrais écrire en français pour que j'en prenne l'abitude.

Ce soir au diner maman et papa m'ont parlé de mon oncle Henri, qui était docteur. Il a maintenant 84 ans. Au début de sa carière mon père ma dit, il était docteur des services militaires colonial. Il était au Cameroun. Le père d'Albert Camus est mort dans de circonstances similaire personne ne savait où il était enterré. Camus a finalement trouvé le tombeaux, mais dès qu'il se trouvait devant, il ne savait pas quoi faire.
