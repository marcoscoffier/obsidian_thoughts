## [[1994-06-06_Goodbye-to-Katherine|Katherine Leaves & Emotional Restraint]]
*1994-06-06 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 29 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p048.jpg|p. 48]]*

Well, Katherine left today. I choked as I tried to say "Bye, Babe." I came so close to crying as I walked back to Daria's from the curb, from where Lisa's blue station wagon pulled Katherine's ever-smiling face away from me. I can't remember ever having felt so natural and happy with someone. 

*(Later annotation: That kind of statement is always problematic—I wonder if it will sound like the 10th grader who wrote about Nira to me someday.)*

I always wonder why I never cry. Do I rationalize my way out of it, or am I stunted emotionally? If I am deprived of the crying extreme, I must also be kept from extreme happiness. I know I don't explode into the exuberant, unrestrained laughter that Katherine does. I love that about her; she is often as emotionally free as a 5-year-old, her voice sliding through octaves and registers over which she has as little control as her toddler counterpart.

I want to start to write regularly, so that I may work out the kinks and awkwardnesses of transcribing my thoughts. I want to try to get this process as free as Katherine's laughter, and as that loose painting stroke she so appreciates.

## [[1994-06-15_Letter-to-Edo-Part-1-Shared-Experience|Letter to Edo (Part 1 — Shared Experience)]]
*1994-06-15 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 18 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p027.jpg|p. 27]]*

1) I'm not talking for Mom, or using Mom's words. Mom & I are very similar, her outlook is in many respects very similar to my own.
- You have no idea how much you are hurting her.

You are so critical of everyone, I can only imagine how critical you are of yourself. I believe that one's *[crossed out: your]* life doesn't really mean anything unless one has *[crossed out: you have]* shared it with others—the more the better.

- I don't buy this "can't grow under the shade of an oak" shit—the you and Richie lame-out. Whatever I have done, it's no big wup (deal); hundreds of thousands of people have done more, better. The fact is that the world is so absolutely full of fascinating things to learn & experience & then pass on that there can never be enough people to do it. You are only on this earth for so many years. Make the most of it. Take everything that comes your way & embrace it, learn from it, let it change you, don't settle on any revelation—it will get boring. Acid is not some hidden mystery, which will revolutionize the world if people would only try it.

## [[1994-07-27_Hull-Bonding-With-His-Son-Through-Drawing|Hull & His Son Drawing as Equals, Plus Sky Painting Tips (Page 81)]]
*1994-07-27 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 81 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p007.jpg|p. 7]]*

`*` Before coming over Hull went by the comic store to buy his kid some comics that he could read in the car on the way to Baltimore: *"We bond through drawing"* Hull said, *"He loves drawing super-heroes (Thats all I drew when I was young - Hull)"*. I have to find if the high detail multiple reflections is really for me or something more about the gesture — maybe both?

## [[1994-07-28_Turning-From-Science-to-Art-and-Robinson-Crusoe|Turning From Science to Art, Commuters & Robinson Crusoe (Page 85)]]
*1994-07-28 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 85 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p014.jpg|p. 14]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p015.jpg|p. 15]]*

I don't think of my turn away from the sciences as a defeat. I guess I could. But, I saw that I could get a lot more out of the arts. The arts are more readily accessible to what I want to get out of life.

Every once in a while I look around, feeling the power of art in my veins, or looking at the world like an artist, and I wonder what all these other people are doing. Why do they get dressed up to go to meetings where presumably they talk of important interesting things & then fall into the most trivial gossip on the subway or train. If their work is so fascinating, how can they turn off like that. I never turn off. If I had all the free time in the world, this is what I would do—what I did this summer, fill my days satisfying my curiosities, and having a regular schedule of exercise, making & doing. I don't need any more (Robinson Crusoe). People say the only reason artists can survive is that there is a community to support them, that an artist is selfish because he is concerned w/ his own progress (or I am selfish) but this is so wrong b/c if everyone had been artists all along there would be no need for communities (I'm starting to get hokey—why do I get carried away) The "artist" I was talking of developed the house, car, computer etc. I guess there isn't room for only artists in a community. I always get an uneasy feeling in the Village where it seems everyone is an artist. There is so much art making [or: acting] that it doesn't mean that much anymore.

## [[1994-07-29_Lighthouse-Point-with-Amy-and-Literary-Sea-Memory|Pier at Lighthouse Point w/ Amy & Literary Sea Memory (Page 89)]]
*1994-07-29 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · notebook p. 89 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p018.jpg|p. 18]]*

I was standing on the end of a Pier w/ Amy at Lighthouse Point. We were on large rectangular rocks which had obviously been placed there by men but long enough ago that they were covered in thick slimy green and any indentation was filled w/ life and shells. I was reminded of V. Woolf's *"Kew Gardens"*. Some children playing in such a puddle could enlarge it until it became as big as the famous English gardens. I am always reminded of a picture in *Outside* magazine (I should buy a macro adaptor-filter?)

We then got up and faced the wind on the sea. It was very hazy so the sun wasn't coming from any particular direction though it was strong and made us squint. We were talking about literature. "Have you read...?" "No, Have you read...?" All my perceptions of the sea have been formed by novels about a young man who goes off to sea—*Horatio Hornblower*, *Nostromo*—I know there are others but I can't remember their names. My image of the sea has been formed by these books. I've never spent much time near ports, weathered boards, barnacles, heavy ropes & pulleys, wet canvas. I know of these things only through an image I have created in conjunction with the books I have read. These books impregnate the image of any of these objects, say the barnacles on the rocks w/ Amy, with a memory which is not my own, but one in me which was created by these books.

## [[1994-07-31_Text-Distance-Camus-and-Saint-Exupery|Textual Distance, Flight Maps, Camus & Saint-Exupéry]]
*1994-07-31 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p031.jpg|p. 31]]*

## [[1994-08-20_Draft-Letter-to-Katherine|Draft Letter to Katherine: Vocation & Companionship]]
*1994-08-20 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · notebook p. 32 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p054.jpg|p. 54]]*

My language is so tense (my handwriting so small). I feel that things changed so much, I'm not sure how to address you. What are you thinking, feeling? What will your first letter to me be like?

## [[1994-10-05_Kant-and-Batesons-Double-Bind|Kant & Bateson's Double Bind]]
*1994-10-05 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 10 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p015.jpg|p. 15]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p016.jpg|p. 16]]*

Kant articulated a profound problem: the human mind can have no direct, mirror-like knowledge of the objective world, for the object it experiences has already been structured by the subject's own internal organization. The human being knows not the world-in-itself, but rather the world-as-rendered-by-the-human-mind (much like the mediation of the artist, especially with photography).

Substitute "world" for "mother" and "human being" for "child" in Gregory Bateson's "double bind" (which eventually leads a person to become schizophrenic):
1. The human being's relation to the world is one of vital dependency, making it critical to assess the nature of that world accurately.
2. The human mind receives contradictory or incompatible information about its situation with respect to the world, rendering its inner psychological and spiritual sense of things incoherent with scientific meta-communication.
3. Epistemologically, the human mind cannot achieve direct communication with the world.
4. Existentially, the human being cannot leave the field.
