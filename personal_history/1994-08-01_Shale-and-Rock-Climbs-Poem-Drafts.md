# Alpine Hike Poems & Crosshatched Rock-Path Drawing (Page 97)

Poetry drafts and revisions across pages 96v–97, with syllable counts in the margins, followed by numbered stanzas and an Alpine landscape pen drawing:

### Working Drafts (scan_1994_2_p034)
**②**
I cause shale to tingle downwards (7)
and sing with the sun (5)
as I climb to the point. (5)

Like the men who fought nature to make
the ski lifts and avalanche barriers
I have made it to the point.

**①**
The ski lifts, roads and paths 6
Mix w/ my steps 4
to scar the earth below me. 7

---

How do you know that I am not clo[ser]
I am closer to the beginning
before nature 4
could be taught out of me
said the student
But you must learn to speak
said the master.

*(Revised right column):*
The student is
Closer to the beginning 7
when nature can't be 6
taught
The master
learning to speak 6 [9]
but then must
Continue to forever learn. 11 [8]

---

I gingerly pull myself up on rocks 10
that have untouched 6
for centuries 4

*(Bracketed revision):*
Rocks balance untouched for centuries 9
Gingerly I climb up on them. 8

---

Hard rock or soft dirt,
The wind and rain 8
and sun loosen what they
drag to the valley (9)
what gravity must drag to the valley

---

*(Boxed haiku & note on Katherine):*
> Best to pick them (10)
> early in the morning, the snowpeas (7)
> cool in my fingers.
> *Mrs. C. Kamegaya.*

`-> I think Katherine is a tactile person.`

---

Flat, bulbous, grey, blue or white 8
all the clouds rush forward on the same wind. 10
The wind and flies 8
I hear and must wait afraid to rupture the silence.

The sun is low its shadows long 8
It pops a drop on my cheek
and I am blinded.

Partly cloudy days are my
favorite, when the sun & the wind
must fight to warm or cool.
