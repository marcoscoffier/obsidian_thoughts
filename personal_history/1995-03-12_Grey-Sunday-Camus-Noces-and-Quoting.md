# Grey Sunday: Camus' Noces, and Quoting

3/12 <u>Grey Sunday</u> -
This break - in New Haven w/ out the students - reminds me of this summer. I am actively thinking but now I have the means to <u>do</u>! It is too bad that I only have another week. Then two months till the end.
My interest in space may have begun when Lois Conner told me to stop looking at myself & see the rest of the world at my sophomore art-review. I was very upset by her comment and found it absolutely unjustified at the time. But slowly I left painting humans and then left objects at all → this may seem like a complete departure from humans but rather focusing attention on the world turns it back on us we are in the world this is what we know and are confronted with.

Dans cette splendeur aride, nous avions erré toute la journée. Peu à peu, le vent, à peine senti au début de l'après-midi, semblait grandir avec les heures et <u>remplir tout le paysage</u> [?] Il soufflait depuis une trouée entre les montagnes, loin vers l'est, accourait du fond de l'horizon et venait bondir en cascades parmi les pierres et le soleil. Sans arrêt, il sifflait avec force à travers les ruines, tournait dans un cirque de pierres et de terre, `[circled:]` baignait les amas de blocs grêlés, entourait chaque colonne de son souffle et venait se répandre en cris incessants sur `[boxed:]` le forum qui s'ouvrait dans le ciel. Je me sentais claquer au vent comme une `[circled:]` mâture. Creusé par le milieu, les yeux brûlés, les lèvres craquantes, ma peau se desséchait jusqu'à ne <u>plus être mienne</u>. I have this terrible problem when quoting, that I want to recreate for my reader the effect that the passage first had on me - I of course read it unedited from the whole book - when I try to quote I ~~[?]~~ am continually battling w/ the tendency to include more and more of the author's text - showing how one thing lead to another to the part that I got excited about. Editing or removing a passage from its context affects it so drastically. It transforms it really. This transformation is mine like choosing parts of the world to frame in my mirror.
I am very excited about my stand - I also had the idea to make a viewer through pipes each one would have a very different color and pick a very different piece of the world to highlight. - <u>shiny metal</u> random what it would choose - colors always changing

Ce bain violent de soleil et de vent épuisait toutes mes forces de vie. À peine en moi ce `[circled, joined by an arrow to the earlier "battements":]` battement d'ailes qui affleure, cette vie qui se plaint, cette faible révolte de l'esprit. Bientôt, répandu aux quatre coins du monde, `[boxed:]` <u>oublieux, oublié</u> de moi-même, <u>je suis ce vent et dans le vent</u>, ces colonnes et cet arc, ces dalles qui sentent chaud et ces montagnes pâles autour de la ville déserte. Et <u>jamais je n'ai senti, si avant</u>, <u>à la fois mon détachement de moi-même et ma présence au monde</u>.

> [!note] Transcription notes
> One sitting, with the journal writing and the quotations interleaved over two pages: the *Noces* passage breaks off at the foot of page 49 ("ses dents") and picks up partway down page 50 ("de givre"), with the paragraph on quoting and the stand sketches written in between, and the margin note "writing is the learning of traces - evidence of" carries on in the last line of page 50. Both quotations are from Camus, "Le Vent à Djémila", in *Noces* (1938). Lois Conner is a photographer who taught at Yale. "The Unbeliever" is a character in Bunyan's *The Pilgrim's Progress*; the line about sleeping on the top of a mast is from Proverbs 23:34 and is quoted in Bunyan. "nier - Noces / Envers - endroit" pairs *Noces* with *L'Envers et l'endroit*, as in [[1995-02-12_Camus-Opposites]]. The last word of the left-margin note is cut off.
