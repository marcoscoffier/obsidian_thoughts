# Pier at Lighthouse Point w/ Amy & Literary Sea Memory (Page 89)

I was standing on the end of a Pier w/ Amy at Lighthouse Point. We were on large rectangular rocks which had obviously been placed there by men but long enough ago that they were covered in thick slimy green and any indentation was filled w/ life and shells. I was reminded of V. Woolf's *"Kew Gardens"*. Some children playing in such a puddle could enlarge it until it became as big as the famous English gardens. I am always reminded of a picture in *Outside* magazine (I should buy a macro adaptor-filter?)

We then got up and faced the wind on the sea. It was very hazy so the sun wasn't coming from any particular direction though it was strong and made us squint. We were talking about literature. "Have you read...?" "No, Have you read...?" All my perceptions of the sea have been formed by novels about a young man who goes off to sea—*Horatio Hornblower*, *Nostromo*—I know there are others but I can't remember their names. My image of the sea has been formed by these books. I've never spent much time near ports, weathered boards, barnacles, heavy ropes & pulleys, wet canvas. I know of these things only through an image I have created in conjunction with the books I have read. These books impregnate the image of any of these objects, say the barnacles on the rocks w/ Amy, with a memory which is not my own, but one in me which was created by these books.
