# Turning From Science to Art, Commuters & Robinson Crusoe (Page 85)

I don't think of my turn away from the sciences as a defeat. I guess I could. But, I saw that I could get a lot more out of the arts. The arts are more readily accessible to what I want to get out of life.

Every once in a while I look around, feeling the power of art in my veins, or looking at the world like an artist, and I wonder what all these other people are doing. Why do they get dressed up to go to meetings where presumably they talk of important interesting things & then fall into the most trivial gossip on the subway or train. If their work is so fascinating, how can they turn off like that. I never turn off. If I had all the free time in the world, this is what I would do—what I did this summer, fill my days satisfying my curiosities, and having a regular schedule of exercise, making & doing. I don't need any more (Robinson Crusoe). People say the only reason artists can survive is that there is a community to support them, that an artist is selfish because he is concerned w/ his own progress (or I am selfish) but this is so wrong b/c if everyone had been artists all along there would be no need for communities (I'm starting to get hokey—why do I get carried away) The "artist" I was talking of developed the house, car, computer etc. I guess there isn't room for only artists in a community. I always get an uneasy feeling in the Village where it seems everyone is an artist. There is so much art making [or: acting] that it doesn't mean that much anymore.
