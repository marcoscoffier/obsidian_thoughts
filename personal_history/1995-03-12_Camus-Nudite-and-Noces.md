# Camus' "Nudité" and Noces

Camus' "nudité" made me imagine walking naked in the desert, maybe this summer. It is funny that one is never struck by the nudity of a picture of a naked woman in the sand The naked woman is "familiar" centuries of depiction When I see the weston I do not think of what that model must have felt lying in the sand - how the sun felt on her skin or the sand under her body the experience is absolutely flattened.
Trying to include the picture taker, the me, rather than the you in the image - I did that alot this summer w/ the shadows, & reflections of myself cropping up in the world - I was there feeling it It cannot exist on its own The tree

**Next page:**

falling in the forest makes no noise unless I am there to hear it.
..."j'aime trop la vie pour ne pas être égoïste." - Camus <u>Noces</u> p. 40
I read Camus and I am so excited I cannot sit still I want to jump up and experience the world - go to Algiers and see the bronzed bodies and feel my skin change color as the sun beats deeper into the month of August. His art drives one directly back into the world one almost does not care to finish the chapter, but rather if it wasn't a chilly night in early March in New Haven but rather a sunny summer day near a beach, I would drop the book and rush outside. Even so I suddenly feel excited to go for a jog.
à mesure qu'on avance dans le mois d'août et que le soleil grandit, le blanc des maisons se fait plus aveuglant et les peaux prennent une chaleur plus sombre. Comment alors ne pas s'identifier à ce dialogue de la pierre et de la chair <u>à la mesure du soleil et des saisons</u>? - Noces "L'été à Alger" - p. 53
