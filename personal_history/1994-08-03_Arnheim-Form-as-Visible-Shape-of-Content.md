# Arnheim: Form as Content, Illusion, Equivalence & Spatial Images (Pages 102–102v)

Notes from *Art & Visual Perception* **pp. 80, 96, 98, 105, 107, 130, 132** paired with Marco's annotations and spatial installation thought:

### Summary of Arnheim Excerpts (pp. 80–132)
- **p. 80 (Single vs. narrative images):** Contrasts a film man pushing through a crowd vs. a still photograph where the artist must rupture still verticals visually: `"Still verticals ruptured by triangular shape?..."`
- **p. 96 (`"Form is the visible shape of content"` — Ben Shahn):** Summarizes how external shape, color, and behavior express inner nature (tea cup vs. knife; rabbit), Wittgenstein's drawn triangle seen as geometric figure, mountain, wedge, or pointer, and marble sculpture referencing living forms rather than stone. Includes Marco's contemporary addition:
  > `[Modern sculpture is more aware that the material speaks about the object — Ron Jones' sculpture made out of wood which it is illegal to export from South Africa, about S. Africa. Sand from..., each w/ its own colors etc.]`
- **Critique of "false naturalness" (pp. 96–97):** Notes Dürer's rabbit, Classic Maya stylized imagery, and Goethe's remark about transforming artifice into nature (`[Goethe's quote seems to sound like Zeuxsis or Giotto comments... I think I see what Arnheim is getting at b/c of the Maya reference above, but the Goethe quote is anything but clear.]`), plus Marco questioning Arnheim's wording on why a "draftsman" depicts what he knows (`Arny often falls into language traps... what are these rules?`).
- **pp. 98, 105, 107, 130, 132 (Projection, Equivalence & Rotating Shadows):** Summarizes that image-making renders an observed equivalent within a medium's properties rather than mechanical optical projection (`[à la grid sketch]`), explains perceptual constancy vs. retinal amoebic shifts (and Marco's note: `[This means that when a person sees a rotating shadow on a flat sheet he very well may see the precept conceived by the brain in 3D...]`), contrasts mobile film/stage sequences against frozen painting (`p. 105`, Marco noting: `again Arnheim's language becomes troublesome. I think it might serve me best to take ideas from him w/out quoting.`), notes `2D = translation of 3D p. 107`, merging partial impressions (`p. 130`), and modern artists recovering the innocent eye against fleeting glimpses (`p. 132`).

### Marco's Closing Question (p. 102v)
- `- I return to my idea of creating a place by a bunch of images hung in spacial relation to each other which repeats the spacial relation the objects represented had to each other. Is this taking art forward or backwards?`
