# Mapping the Sun

I thought the other day at sun-set that the sun's rays act like a lever at sun-set they find the highest point and pivot around it, or the edge of the earth if it is flat.
`[sketch: a small circle labelled "setting sun", a line from it grazing the top of a larger circle (the earth)]`
`[sketch: a hill in profile, one slope hatched; labels "rosy sun-set lit" and "in shade"]`
`[sketch: a square with a hatched triangle of shadow, labelled "angle changes w/ day." and "build a wall ⊥ to sun's rays."]`
`[sketch: two curved, hatched surfaces facing each other, labelled sideways "Bunch of transparent scrims - catch sun @ diff. times of the year. <u>Sun mapping</u>"]`
I saw the blue again & it is <u>so</u> easy
\- no longer purely optical
& Turrell's about daydream spaces
→ `[in a loop:]` <u>Mapping the sun</u> | <u>Robert Gordon</u> → place the stand in diff. spots for the sun-set.
\- St.Ex about being on that flat mesa.
\- mirrors scattered on the ground. around the hill
`[sketch: a line of horizon with small squares on it, labelled "set sun hill"]`
put shadow fla[g?] `[cut off by the scan]`
