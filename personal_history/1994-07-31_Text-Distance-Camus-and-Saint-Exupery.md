# Textual Distance, Flight Maps, Camus & Saint-Exupéry

Text allows one to travel through space or concieve of space in the same way that one concieves of it normally by adding up a sequence of stills and logically concieving — that mountain is far b/c it took me 3 hrs to walk up to the end of the grass over there and that looks like only about one inch from here. But this system is highly inexact and operates only by comparison. When one flies one can barely concieve of the distances that are being covered b/c one imagines a map — or the Swiss-air flight I was on w/ TV's in the cabins that show a map w/ a plane on it showing you where you are.

Camus concieves of such distances in the beginning of *"Le premier homme"* & Exupéry in *Le Vol de Nuit* when the pilot Fabien talks of the towns.

### Copied French Passages (Summarized)
- **Albert Camus (*Le Premier Homme*):** Transcribes an opening passage describing thick dusk clouds sweeping eastward over a stony cart track after gathering over the Atlantic, racing across the Moroccan crests, high Algerian plateaus, and the Tunisian border toward the Tyrrhenian Sea—faster than empires across millennia—until they dissolve into large sparse raindrops pattering onto the canvas hood above the four travelers (`[et il revient aux passagers]`).
- **Antoine de Saint-Exupéry (*Vol de Nuit*):** Transcribes Fabien's aerial view of hills carving wakes of shadow into evening gold while luminosity rises from the plains like winter snow.
