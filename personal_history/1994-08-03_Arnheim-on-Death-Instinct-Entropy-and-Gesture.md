# Arnheim: Anti-Entropy, Musical Scale & Double Process of Seeing (Page 101)

*Art & Visual Perception* quotes & Marco's bracketed commentary (`[...]`):

### Summary of Arnheim Excerpts (pp. 17, 37, 40, 44)
- **p. 17 (Perceived image as the artwork):** Notes Arnheim's insistence that the perceived image—not physical pigment—is the work of art: perceptual forces pulling an unbalanced disk `[center-square sketch]` are artistically real even if physically "illusory."
- **p. 37 (Critique of Freud's death instinct & economy principle):** Summarizes Arnheim's rejection of Freud's claim that organisms strive toward inorganic rest—arguing instead that healthy human life fulfills itself in doing, moving, growing, and creating, waging an uphill battle against entropy.
  - Marco's marginal additions inside brackets:
    > `[Freud] assigned a fundamental role to the "death instinct,"... Man is lazy by nature [some men maybe. But maybe the most active find that life is easiest in activity. It is much easier for the marathoner to run 25 miles if his car breaks down, than to sit by the road and try to flag someone down]`
    > `[grass and trees are the only things which keep the rocks and dirt from falling off a mountain. They have to fight against the wind, sun, rain, melting snow & freezing ice which all loosen the rocks & dirt & send them down, closer to the center of gravity of the planet.]` — p. 37.
- **p. 40 (Music & Image — composition):** Compares the musical scale and framed visual composition in both combining rising intensity from base to peak with symmetry returning to a base/rest.
- **p. 44 (Gesture & caricature):** Summarizes Arnheim on seeing essential structural features (blueness of sky, curve of swan's neck, simple face strokes, Köhler's chimpanzees startled by button eyes, caricature catching a person's likeness through few lines). Closes with Marco's added note:
  > `- making art is the double process of seeing; then of making others see.` — p. 44.
