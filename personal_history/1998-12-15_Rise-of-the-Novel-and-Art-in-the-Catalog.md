# Rise of the Novel, and Art in the Catalog

In the <u>Rise of the Novel</u>. the writer speaks of how literacy induces <u>aloneness</u>. In reading a book I enter a separate world, a world where certain issues become more important than others - there is a type of modern art which is more important and more interesting in a catalog than in the <u>real</u>. Almost in the way that a photograph can make a moment - a catalog can make an artwork ~~[?]~~ Hal Foster says that Stella or another artist in the 70's separates the sign from the signified but this is an action designated by the critic. I wonder how conscious the artist actually is: In the case of many, I doubt there is any consciousness at all → of his relationship w/ the sign

> [!note] Transcription notes
> Undated. *The Rise of the Novel* is Ian Watt's 1957 study. Hal Foster is the art critic.
