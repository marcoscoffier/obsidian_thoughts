# The Other Vertigo: Noces and Hitchcock

Confronting the other Vertigo → wish to drop into what can never be a part of yourself.
\- rare moments of extasy like in <u>Noces</u>: breathing with the world
writing leaves the tracings of the ephemeral flickerings of the mind → like the tracings of the wind on Camus' skin in "Le vent a Djémila"
"l'ecriture du monde" - perfect.
corps = dessin → world has taken over his powers, he is losing himself
`[sketch: two small headlamps]` two beams of a headlight shine and though starting from two specific points the wash of light can meld
<u>Noces</u> is a book about Unity hence the title → extatic union
p. 66-7. [he also talks of religion → Genet or <u>L'Etranger</u> → decision
Artist cannot simply live. self-consciousness has ripped him from this bliss.
`[boxed, with a bracket in the margin:]` "Vivre, bien sûr, c'est un peu le contraire d'exprimer." - p. 78. "Le désert".
↳ why can Camus <u>not simply</u> live like the Algerians?
At the opening of Hitchcock's <u>Vertigo</u> Scottie our protagonist starts w/ hands grasping a bar: the tenuousness of life.
the hands are that of the man our protagonist Scottie (Jimmy Stewart) is chasing. In the chase over the roof tops ~~Scottie~~ Scottie slips but manages to grasp onto a very unstable gutter. His partner reaches ~~[?]~~ to help him but plunges to his death "He is in terror but at the same time transfixed. He cannot help but peer into his awesome predicament below ... Hitchcock confronts us with life's fragility, with the fear (yet fascination) with what lies beyond, and with the overwhelming danger (ie. deadliness) of tangling with that reality.
