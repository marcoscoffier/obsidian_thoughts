## [[1994-07-30_Clouds-Screens-Colored-Filters-and-Shadow-Rooms|Cloud Spaces, Screens & Colored Filter Shadow Room]]
*1994-07-30 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p021.jpg|p. 21]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p022.jpg|p. 22]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p023.jpg|p. 23]]*

I was excited by the feeling I got in the plane when we were inside the cloud layers. I thought right away of ways I could recreate such a feeling in a sculpted space.

My first idea was rather basic: room full of cloud like materials which the viewer can pass through. *[Sketch of cube room]* I would especially like the effect of passing through a cloud, not under, over or around a cotton representation of one—that is a marvelous feature of clouds, that one can never experience their surface, it does not exist up close, only from afar. Even if a parachuter jumped from the plane onto the soft white billowing surface he would go right through and never be quite sure at what point he passed through what he had thought was a surface. If someone in a plane took a movie of his jump they could approximate maybe even name a point to a certain degree of accuracy at which he actually penetrated that surface, but everything in which I am interested lies in that degree of accuracy.

**My second idea (warmer):**
When you look at it from afar, it looks like a solid wall — but when you approach it you realize that it is actually possible to pass right through.
> *[Diagrams: `Front` square view of wave/horizon strips -> `Side` staggered vertical hanging strips; `I could maybe achieve such an effect with glass.` -> `Front` horizon panel -> angled layered glass partitions & snaking visitor path diagram; `more layers would probably be needed to have this effect at all`]*

- What about the wood slats?... `<- SCREENS!` — ocean waves.
- This is about the excitement I had about layers, textures on top *[crosshatch sketch]* — ocean waves w/ wind, earth layers evident in mountains etc & even about Erica's photographs of the chalk boards which have their own kind of history of layered sediment (marks representing thought or all kinds of diagrams really—which stimulate thought?)
- The beginning of *Le Premier Homme*.

I remember when I was much younger, erasing all the smudges and stray marks off of a drawing I had done & not liking it anymore, it became stale to me. I then remember making drawing where I would intentionally smudge things and leave stray marks because I wanted it to have the same feel as the drawing I had ruined by cleaning up. I didn't like this second drawing either because the smudges etc. were contrived & didn't add to the life of the drawing (`; (these are words` `[unclear/edge clipped] now I wouldn't have used them`).

##### Translucent Slats, RGB Filters & the Black Wall
The shadows would multiply & get complicated in the crossings of slated planes, it could become dense enough that it would be impossible to see through, or completely dark in the back from a lighted front.

Layers of plastic of different colors could create complete blackness. It would require a passage through these films to realize that the piece was made up of many colored translucent sheets. I just screwed [my] red & green filters together which created a beautiful brown (warm)—I would need blue to make it black (or grey). I could not tell, once screwed together which side was red & which green. I took them apart and held them in line about an inch apart so that I saw only brown through one eye, and noticed that the light, which was on an angle cast the shadows of green & red of the individual filters — might be possible to make a thick frame around each one which would make it impossible to see the color of the gels except by the shadows that would be cast — I might build a special shadow capturer.
> *[Diagram: `light ->` shining at an angle across four framed colored sheets (`looks black`) casting separated `rainbow shadows` below.]*

- w/ my room w/ lattice layers, shall I paint the back wall black? Or... just got a great idea, viewer enters the dark area sees black wall but as he works his way through to the front, it is illuminated by a large window, and the visual effect from the beginning is no longer evident, it has been made evident.

## [[1994-07-31_Alps-Space-Stereoscopic-Boxes-and-Sound-Bike-Room|Alps Space, Stereoscopic Boxes & Sound-Bike Octagon Room (Page 93)]]
*1994-07-31 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · notebook p. 93 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p029.jpg|p. 29]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p030.jpg|p. 30]]*

**Visual experience in Literature beyond the ekphrastic.**

A 2D picture traps space w/in it. the viewer is kept from experiencing the space in which the photographer, or painter, was standing by the edges of his image. The edges, the frame defines it as an image but the viewer cannot fly through the space on that flat plane; He is constantly grounded by the frame. It is impossible for me to convey my feeling about the Alps on paper with photo emulsion, pencil or paint b/c that experience has everything to do with the feeling of space I experience in the mountains.

`((` Writers can tap into this space b/c their medium is narrative. In order to experience space as space, one has to move through it. The visual experience of a flat image is static in time & space. One cannot move through it, into it, but only around it. It is frozen in time.

I did a sculpture project about stereoscopic vision last semester and the culmination of a series of investigations was a set of boxes where I cut up "Stereoscopic" photographs — I had taken 2 views of a still-life, right eye/left eye, I xeroxed one onto acetate on a color copier, then pasted the clear one over its partner. — I cut these photographs by object and placed them in a box at the relative distances these objects had had in the original still life. *[Cube box sketch with layered cutout planes inside]* I was left w/ a lot of empty space btw. these slices of picture. There was no way of looking at the box so that the pictures would come back together as one.

Maybe it would be possible to make such a box, the viewer would have to be directed towards the particular initial viewing spot — sees flat image. Then, when he approaches it, he realizes that they are forced into space.

## [[1994-08-04_Cubism-Acetate-Layers-Distance-and-Flashlight-Room|Cubism, Acetate Layers, Perceived Distance & Flashlight Dark Room (Pages 103–103v)]]
*1994-08-04 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · notebook p. 103 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p045.jpg|p. 45]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p046.jpg|p. 46]]*

Notes from Arnheim (**pp. 132–133**) followed by Marco's observations of spatial distance and dark-room lighting installations:

##### Cubism Summary & Acetate Transparency Flip-Book (p. 103)
- Summarizes Arnheim (pp. 132–133) arguing against the notion that Cubism expects viewers to fly mentally around an object from multiple angles—instead, clashing aspects exist simultaneously on a flat plane.
- `- Thought of painting "sections" of the world on clear acetate, when you piled the transparencies, you would create a kind of 3 dimensions. Flip-book; looks like 2D image untill you start removing the layers, and revealing what had been hidden by an object further in the foreground. Do this to a master painting?`

##### Inducing 3D Space: Mary, Julien & Katrine, Annecy & Childe Hassam (p. 103)
There must be many ways of enducing the viewer's mind to form 3D mental precept — which is his mind's normal way of viewing the world. I always think of Mary (librarian at work) sitting at the other side of the circular lunch table *[Sketch of circular table and figure]* — that large flat plane took up the entire bottom half of my vision / I was very aware of the space between me & her.

Yesterday I sat on the very flat lawn and was looking at the space between Julien and Katrine's bodies, I was very aware of that distance, more so than would be possible by a drawing which included the old couple (smaller than their heads) *[Sketch of Julien & Katrine in foreground with tiny figures behind]*. Looking at the sparkling sun on the water `[p. 101]` that distance was made evident by the pattern (for which word exists in French) of lights which got tighter & brighter as it got farther away, but Julien & the grass, Mary at the table & my Chapel York painting of the flat parking lot aren't about pattern, just distance. Childe Hassam's ocean painting at the YUAG doesn't invoke distance as much as it brings attention to the texture which make the waves — the brightest lights are made by reflections on the surface texture of the paint. One would have to check this by moving the light or the painting around.

##### Flashlight Dark Room & Mirror-House Space (pp. 103–103v)
I thought of a very dark space where the viewer would have to bring his own light — carry a flashlight. He could investigate textures, spaces by his own ray of light, the textures their own mean. — move light source = composite images. Create single illusions w/ "place light here signs" — but he travels w/ his light to each individual viewing spot.

I thought of my transparent mirror sculpture w/ a hole underneath for the viewer's flashlight — force him to investigate on his own.
- could create effects w/ mirrors, his light bouncing off a mirror — like walking in the mirror house at Nat'l Gallery of Art, viewer had to move into space to sound the difference btw. mirrored bed & mirror chair on mirror floor.

I am controlling his viewing & making him see, he does not see till he shines his light on an object. —
