## [[1994-06-15_Letter-to-Edo-Part-2-Yale-and-Individuation|Letter to Edo (Part 2 — Yale & Individuation)]]
*1994-06-15 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 19 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p028.jpg|p. 28]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p029.jpg|p. 29]]*

Question everything, which is so in your nature it sounds silly to tell you this—I have learned from you to question many things I may have taken for granted.

I get slammed every day at Yale—that's how I'm learning. Let yourself get slammed, admit that you have been slammed in front of a huge crowd and fucked up even though you were trying your hardest. Know that you did the best you could, and sucked. It doesn't mean you suck. Find out why people thought you sucked and you learn more, quicker, than any other way. It's very easy to convince yourself, with any group of people that love you or are your friends, that you are great.

Someday I hope that whatever decision you make in your life, you would be able to come back to anyone that thought you were doing the wrong thing and prove that they were wrong—to show them up, whoever they are.

Mom objects to drugs. You choose to use them for whatever reasons—they make you happy, more receptive to music. Prove to Mom that it's your right decision. It's not going to happen if you hang around burnouts... Believe me, you are so fucking smart you surprise me. I would count you smarter, more perceptive, and more constantly thinking than 90% of the people at Yale. Why would Yale not accept you? Believe me, it's not that you couldn't handle it.

## [[1994-07-09_Drawing_on_the_Green_w_Alison|Drawing on the New Haven Green with Alison]]
*1994-07-09 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 59 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p102.jpg|p. 102]]*

I also went to Jazz on the Green with Alison Henyey and started drawing the crowd -- which I kind of like to do. After a while a little boy sat down to watch and three little girls joined. THere was the running commentary of ... " what is he drawing now? " " the trees, don't you see? " It was kind of interesting to see what they recognized as what. They didn't pick up on the the gesture drawings of the people very well ( maybe they were too sloppy or it was dark) but he (the boy was really perceptive and looking right over my shoulder) saw windows and trees right away. I then asked them if I could draw them. The got really nervous whenever they could see I was getting near their faces, and so did I. I didn't want to let them down, hence I didn't draw their faces very well. When I finally got to the boy, I could get him at all. He had been so attentive. I did a really quick sketch when he wasn't looking and I liked that better --- so did one of the little girls!

## [[1994-07-09_Yale-as-a-Trial-by-Fire|Yale as a Trial by Fire]]
*1994-07-09 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 59 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p102.jpg|p. 102]]*

I wonder if this last statement can be challenged. My time at Yale has made me fearful—like a war survivor. I expect shots from all directions, especially from where I least expect them, whenever I make any kind of statement. This is good, I guess. One can always argue against any kind of trial by fire—it teaches the quickest, like my experience with Jen Mayer—but it can also destroy the insecure. The Marine type says, "Great, only the tough will survive and they will be even tougher"—Darwinism. But the Union member screams discrimination when a learning disabled person is denied a job in the library system. -- I'm rambling.

## [[2026-11-11_Yale-Meat-Market-and-Cibone-Show|The Yale Meat Market & Design Show at Cibone]]
*2026-11-11 · Present, Therapy Notes*

I think what "grosses me out" about the girls who would flock to preppy Yale singers is that they were deluded by an image, a dream or hope, without knowing the person inside. The worst guys would take advantage of the power imbalance to debase and abuse these confused girls. The whole meat market grossed me out. I felt powerless to change it and I didn’t want to be involved. And yet still today I am thrown into the Yale preppy stereotype. Bret Kavanaugh and Chris Cuomo are despicable yet hold highly visible positions of power and influence. I have shame for those times I might have exerted a power.

I went to a design show at Cibone with Miwa—so much posturing and posing. No interesting conversations. I feel so much distance to the fashionable. I can’t feel excited by the moods or the expensive objects—even the carefully handmade ones. Not in that vibe. I always feel so looked at, so judged. I make the wrong laughable choice. All this style is such a fad.

We had dinner alone with Miwa afterwards. She said she was exhausted by all the social tension. I felt a bit closer to her, but with all the roiling tension I have inside, still very far.
