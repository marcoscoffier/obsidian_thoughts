## [[1994-07-26_Hulls-Knife-Edge-Between-Artist-and-Nature|Hull's Knife Edge Between the Painter and Nature]]
*1994-07-26 · Yale, New Haven · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p004.jpg|p. 4]]*

Hull mentioned in class yesterday—about having a relationship w/ nature—no relationship is interesting if one party always wins—the relationship will either end altogether or be terribly unhappy.

The relationship of a painter w/ nature is that between that which is going on in his head—gestures, expression & that which he gathers from nature. If nature always wins (trompe l'oeil [or] other forms of direct copying)—then the work gets boring—as does Abstract expressionism or another form which is all about the artist. I like to try to walk this knife edge, mixing the gesture, small recognitions or reminiscences w/ a careful comparison to what I see before me.

## [[1994-07-27_Dialogues-with-Nature-Smoke-Fly-and-Squirrels|Dialogues with Nature: Smoke, Fly & Squirrels (7/27)]]
*1994-07-27 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 80 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p004.jpg|p. 4]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p005.jpg|p. 5]]*

**7/27** — I remember several dialogues with nature:

I was lying on my back in the Saybrook grass court yard listening to Dvořák's cello concerto. There was a flume of smoke rising from the dark roof, illuminated by the moon which was full, and close behind it. The plume seemed to dance as it rose. It would go from heavy to almost non-existent. The Music was happening inside my head between the two earpieces of my headphones & the plume was outside in the cool night. There were times when the plume seemed to react to the music I was listening to; almost vanish with a decrescendo or dance wildly in a more vigorous part. Those moments of syncrony were wonderful and random, and wonderful for being random.

I had the same sort of feeling watching a fly in my room. For some reason flies circle around in the very center of the room where a string hangs down from the ceiling light. Its motions were generally much quicker than the smoke's had been, but it too seemed to get some kind of rhythm or pattern from the music.

I will never forget a wonderful day at the end of exams when I went for a long run & lay down under a chestnut tree near the Divinity school. Two squirrels were playing or flirting near me & at times it seemed I understood what they were communicating to each other and they felt my presence in their world.

I think it is interesting to think of these interior/exterior running dialogues. When are they synchronized? Can they be synchronized? or are we just experiencing random connection patterns which happen to seem similar?

## [[1994-08-12_Sculpture-Garden-and-West-Rock-Cliff|The Sculpture Garden & West Rock Cliff]]
*1994-08-12 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 70 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p122.jpg|p. 122]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p123.jpg|p. 123]]*

After a long exposed walk down the alley that runs by Skull & Bones and leads only to the bronze gate to the sculpture garden, I pull out my key and slip into a dark corridor. Turning right into an unroofed space—walls of dark burgundy shooting upwards like a medieval European street—a narrow band of sunlight illuminates the entire right-hand wall with orange brightness, picking out every bit of textural detail in the stone while the left wall remains in impenetrable blackness. Past the Henry Moore statue and the lantern, I find the shade of a Louise Nevelson wall made of bronze boxes to put on my climbing shoes.

I quit rugby two years ago because I wanted to combine my athletic endeavors with some connection to nature. I will always remember one of my first days mountain biking up West Rock. I pushed through a small tree and some bushes and found myself, bike in hand, on the edge of the cliff facing the sunset. Below me was a massive lake, with a tiny boat and a sole fisher on its bright surface. I was 25 minutes from my dorm room in dirty, motorcycle- and fire-truck-ridden New Haven, and I couldn't hear a thing, let alone see a house.

Dad has taught me something I will cherish and enjoy the rest of my life: there is something beautiful and necessary in the immensity of nature. Reading about James Turrell and Count Panza makes me believe there can be art which evokes this feeling—a feeling found in the bowl of mountain and sky.
