## [[1994-08-03_Arnheim-on-Overall-Structure-and-Form-Meaning|Arnheim: Overall Structure, Simplicity & 'A Whole Maintains Itself' (Pages 101–101v)]]
*1994-08-03 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · notebook p. 101 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p041.jpg|p. 41]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p042.jpg|p. 42]]*

Notes from *Art & Visual Perception* **pp. 44, 45, 62, 67–68** alongside Marco's self-reflection on reading and writing:

##### Summary of Arnheim Excerpts
- **pp. 44–45 (*Detail*):** Summarizes anthropologists' finding that people unfamiliar with photography struggle to read "realistic" photos that Western viewers have learned to decipher, and that overall structural features (such as triangularity) are primary perceptual data rather than late abstractions.
- **p. 62 (*Simplicity — form & meaning in image & language*):** Summarizes Arnheim's argument that every artwork conveys meaning about existence (like a teapot interpreting its function to the eye), so simplicity depends on matching visual/verbal structure to intended thought—short sentences do not guarantee simplicity if form conflicts with meaning.
- **pp. 67–68 (*A Whole Maintains Itself | GESTALT*):** Summarizes how elements in a visual field depend on total context yet are selectively affected; contrasts physical wholes (water vs. rock, Michelangelo's *Madonna* marble unaffected physically by damage) with perceptual interactions in the nervous system (greenish tint of a pale face beside a red dress), citing architect **Eduardo Torroja** on a straight tie-bar curving optically against an arch or rectangle inside an ogive.

##### Marco's Reflection on Reading Arnheim vs. New Critics
> `[The new critics objection to quoting is a concern for the text as a gestalt, whereas it is less of one that an image or an object. One does not always readily comprehend or concieve the major structures of a text. One has to read one word at a time. The structure can help to bring one to an understanding at a certain point. Arnheim himself for all the attempts he has made to structure his work cannot concieve of it as a gestalt. He makes several points a paragraph. I find that the realizations I have made defy the structure of the whole, and that I have liked the process of returning to copy parts I have underlined more than I actually liked reading the chapters. The quotes make almost more sense or resonate more clearly when they are freed from the constraints and confusion of the many various thoughts in this text. I think that I think & write similarly to Arnheim, I have a lot of problems seeing the whole]`
