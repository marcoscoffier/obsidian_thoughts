## [[1994-07-29_Rene-Thom-From-the-Icon-to-the-Symbol|On Plane July 29–30: René Thom's From the Icon to the Symbol]]
*1994-07-29 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p017.jpg|p. 17]]*

**On Plane July 29–30**

Reading notes and bracketed responses on **René Thom's *From the Icon to the Symbol*** and `'L'espace et les signes'` (*Semiotica* 29-3/4 [1981] 193–208):

##### Summary of Thom Passages & Marco's Bracketed Reactions
- **Introducing a new mathematical symbol:** Thom describes how a mathematician juggling recurring expressions introduces a new symbol (creating a new semantic field to break free of "obsessional presences") or predicts properties a priori before constructing expressions. Marco adds:
  > `[How often does this really happen? or has Thom created a good example? Did this happen, say with the creation of the integral symbol?]`
  > `[Feyerabend says this is what always happens]`
- **Infant babbling around 18 months & phonemes:** Thom compares the mathematician babbling before Mother Nature (*bombinans in vacuo* unless able to hear reality's response in the symbol) to a baby initially producing phonemes of every world language before narrowing to the parents' native syntax. Marco objects:
  > `[I find it unfortunate that... Experimenting w/ all the different sounds one can make Bobby Mc.F. picking just those that are possible w/in the confines of one language is, well, rather confining. But those other phonemes sound foreign which unfortunately is often barbaric or terrible sounding, at least to those in the Western cultures.]`
- **Also see: `'L'espace et les signes'` (Semiotica 29-3/4 [1981] 193–208):** Notes Thom's mutual assimilation of "semantic" and "spacial" categories—that the location of a physical object in space and of a signification in semantic space are not only genetically connected but analogous in structure and function.
