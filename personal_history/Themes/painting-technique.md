## [[1994-07-15_Mixing-Paint-on-a-Mirror|Mixing Paint on a Mirror]]
*1994-07-15 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 64 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p110.jpg|p. 110]]*

At work: I was thinking of a painting of a still life which is lit, like the paintings I have been discussing, with the same light illuminating the still life. This is similar to my using a mirror to mix my paints on when I go outside to paint. I mix the paints directly on the sky—I see all the colors in relation to the sky, which reflects throughout the landscape as well.

## [[1994-07-27_Hull-Bonding-With-His-Son-Through-Drawing|Hull & His Son Drawing as Equals, Plus Sky Painting Tips (Page 81)]]
*1994-07-27 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 81 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p007.jpg|p. 7]]*

`*` Before coming over Hull went by the comic store to buy his kid some comics that he could read in the car on the way to Baltimore: *"We bond through drawing"* Hull said, *"He loves drawing super-heroes (Thats all I drew when I was young - Hull)"*. He showed me a small sketch book he had taken to the ballgame with his son. *"He could never sit through a whole game, he doesn't understand it, but if we're drawing he'll stay 3 hrs."* In the book the pages alternated Dad & son. *"With drawing we can be equals, I'm not teaching him anything. We're both artists."* A strange comment coming from a Yale professor. I know he's dying to have his son become an artist which was hard for him & will be for me (if I decide to do it) because it was so foreign to what our parents did. It will be absolutely natural for Hull's son — both his parents are artists, And he will have been drawing the way I would listen to music w/ my mom, he's not even 5 yet. Those memories from before real memory begins are so powerful.

Before I forget, Hull had two tips about painting large flat planes: mix 3 big pots up first of maybe 3 sky colors, to have enough to cover whole sky & maybe I should paint those areas w/ shorter strokes in the quick paintings, I pick up my brush, whereas the longer ones are constantly scumbled. I have to find if the high detail multiple reflections is really for me or something more about the gesture — maybe both?

## [[1994-08-02_Studio-Painting-Ansel-Adams-and-Textures|Studio Painting, Ansel Adams & Mixed Textures]]
*1994-08-02 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p051.jpg|p. 51]]*

I have now realized that all of these painting ideas require a technical prowess that I do not yet have. The technical level has to complement the idea, or the work becomes silly. Who knows, maybe I'll get there?

I thought today that I might actually try to make a painting "in the studio" when I return to New Haven. In the studio the painter is aware of his relationship to the painting; nature is not present. I also thought that setting up a photograph *à la* Ansel Adams is similar to setting up a painting—Polaroid sketches, trying different filters.

I was excited by my first try with textures today. I'm going to definitely try mixing anything with medium: sawdust, fine sand, dirt, grass. Gesso is a kind of adhesive—I wonder if I could glue grass, leaves, or branches onto a surface with it and then paint on it. I also thought of building a diorama-like 3D painting by layering successive levels on receding planes.
