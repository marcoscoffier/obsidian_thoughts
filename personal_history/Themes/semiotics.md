When I called Garth this weekend he wasn't home:
"Hi, this is Marco."
"Hi, it's Amy."
Doesn't this message tell me anything? I did not know of course which Amy it was—the only two girls in the apartment are called Amy. ## [[1994-06-22_Catachresis-and-Quillian-Semiotic-Model|Catachresis, Quillian Semiotic Model & Writing Reflection (Page 39)]]
*1994-06-22 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 39 · scan [[attachments/Screenshot_2026-11-18_at_10.27.18_AM.png|Screenshot_2026-11-18_at_10.27.18_AM]]*

catachreses = metaphor turned into culture
...metaphors tend to resist acquisition (like factual information). If they are inventive (and thus original) they cannot be easily accepted; the system tends not to absorb them.

> *[Bracketed Self-Reflection — Upper Right]:*
> "I am getting very angry at the weaknesses in my writing. I can no longer forgive the misspellings & weak formations, and general looseness I have always forgiven myself because I thought it was a key part of being me—whatever that means."

The Quillian model: a mass of nodes interconnected by different types of associative links. For the meaning of *[continues onto yellow sticky note, left]*: every lexeme, memory should contain a node which has as its 'patriarch' the term to be defined here called *type*. The configuration of the meaning of the lexeme is given by the multiplicity of its links with several tokens each of which becomes, in its turn, a *type B*. Type B is the patriarch of a new configuration.... "The overall structure of the complete memory forms an enormous aggregation of planes, each consisting entirely of token nodes except for its 'head' node." This model therefore anticipates the definition of every sign, thanks to its interconnection with the universe of all other signs that function as its interpretants, each of which is ready to become *[continues onto yellow sticky note, right]* a sign interpreted by all the others. The model, in its complexity, is based on a process of unlimited semiosis. Starting with a sign that is considered a 'type', one can retrace, from the center to the extreme periphery, the entire universe of cultural units. Each of these can in turn become the center and generate infinite peripheries.

## [[1994-07-28_Umberto-Eco-Semantics-of-Metaphor|Umberto Eco: The Semantics of Metaphor (Page 83)]]
*1994-07-28 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 83 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p010.jpg|p. 10]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p011.jpg|p. 11]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p012.jpg|p. 12]]*

Extensive reading notes and bracketed critiques (`[...]`) on **Umberto Eco's *The Semantics of Metaphor*** across notebook pp. 82v–84:

##### Key Concepts Summarized from Eco
- **Semiotic vs. metasemiotic judgments:** Codes do not only produce tautological utterances; they let us form metasemiotic judgments that invent new semantic couplings and call the code itself into question. Every metaphor traces back to a subjacent chain of metonymic connections (even in everyday messages and philosophy, whenever culture must name something unassimilated).
- **Joyce, *Finnegans Wake*, and Picasso (p. 83):** *Finnegans Wake* exemplifies unlimited semiosis through phonetic contiguity preceding semantic similitude. Summarizes Eco on *meander tale* / *Neanderthal* (phonetic + semantic resonance vs. "empty modern art" `'Oh yeah, so?'`) and Picasso's bicycle seat + handlebars forming a bull's head (`"becomes rewarding or does it become trite?"`), plus Eco's multidimensional *Ersatz* field diagram (*Graph p. 259*).
- **Schema of metaphor & catachresis (p. 83v):** `A -> k, k <- D` (`woman -> long white neck <- swan`: calling a woman a swan is metaphor; when it ages into cliché like *neck of a bottle*, it becomes *catachresis*). Contrasts Dante's *"dark wood"*, Achillini's fire-heat-sweat factual comparisons, Galileo's condemnation for *semiotic* rather than logical contradiction with Scripture, and Joyce's *etyma + atom = 'abnihilation of the ethym'* ("before nuclear fission").

## [[1994-07-30_Bateson-Theory-of-Play-and-Fantasy|Notes on Semiotics: Bateson's Theory of Play & Fantasy (Page 90)]]
*1994-07-30 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · notebook p. 90 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p024.jpg|p. 24]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p025.jpg|p. 25]]*

**Notes on Semiotics: Bateson, *Theory of Play & Fantasy***

##### Summary of Bateson Excerpts
- Contrasts involuntary animal mood-signs (sexual odor) with human cosmetics and perfumes worn voluntarily, which nevertheless produce automatic/auto-suggestive effects even on the wearer; links this evolutionary threshold—eating of the Tree of Knowledge to discover that *signals are signals*—to human language, empathy, identification, and multiple levels of abstraction.
- Outlines **metalinguistic** messages (about language, e.g. `"the word 'cat' has no fur and cannot scratch"`) vs. **metacommunicative** messages (about the speakers' relationship, e.g. `"This is play"`, mostly implicit), where `"This is play"` expands to the paradox that these actions do not denote what they would denote.
- Notes that inside a dream, the dreamer lacks the concept `"untrue"` and cannot normally frame the dream, while schizophrenic `"word salad"` omits the `"as if"` frame-setting message, acting out metaphors literally.
