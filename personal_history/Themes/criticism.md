## [[1994-07-09_Drawing_on_the_Green_w_Alison|Drawing on the New Haven Green with Alison]]
*1994-07-09 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 59 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p102.jpg|p. 102]]*

I also went to Jazz on the Green with Alison Henyey and started drawing the crowd -- which I kind of like to do. After a while a little boy sat down to watch and three little girls joined. THere was the running commentary of ... " what is he drawing now? " " the trees, don't you see? " It was kind of interesting to see what they recognized as what. They didn't pick up on the the gesture drawings of the people very well ( maybe they were too sloppy or it was dark) but he (the boy was really perceptive and looking right over my shoulder) saw windows and trees right away. I then asked them if I could draw them. The got really nervous whenever they could see I was getting near their faces, and so did I. I didn't want to let them down, hence I didn't draw their faces very well. When I finally got to the boy, I could get him at all. He had been so attentive. I did a really quick sketch when he wasn't looking and I liked that better --- so did one of the little girls!

## [[1994-07-09_Yale-as-a-Trial-by-Fire|Yale as a Trial by Fire]]
*1994-07-09 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 59 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p102.jpg|p. 102]]*

I wonder if this last statement can be challenged. My time at Yale has made me fearful—like a war survivor. I expect shots from all directions, especially from where I least expect them, whenever I make any kind of statement. This is good, I guess. One can always argue against any kind of trial by fire—it teaches the quickest, like my experience with Jen Mayer—but it can also destroy the insecure. The Marine type says, "Great, only the tough will survive and they will be even tougher"—Darwinism. But the Union member screams discrimination when a learning disabled person is denied a job in the library system. -- I'm rambling.

## [[1995-01-26_Croce-and-the-Undiscussable|Croce and the Undiscussable]]
*1995-01-26 · Final Semester Yale 1995 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1995_p024.jpg|p. 24]]*

1/26 - we talked about Arlene Croce's "Discussing the Undiscussable" NYorker Jan 5 '95 In sculpture all day yesterday. I think that it is quite interesting to ~~compare~~ discuss in referance to the art is a ~~[?]~~ satellite idea. When Cage or DuChamp remove the ~~viewer~~ artist from the work of art. The viewer can 'not care' and nothing exists. Croce removes the viewer from a work which is all artist - she questions whether there is work (art) there at all.
I like the belief that art is about communication, the artist convey's his feelings hoping to hit a resonance. Is a comedian an artist? He has an instant acceptance gauge - laughter.
Can the audience be considered crippling to the true artist? I think the artist should have an audience in mind. He can pick the one he wants. → lover, friend, Muse `[a wavy line under "true"; an arrow from "mind." leads to a question mark]`

> [!note]- Transcription notes
> Arlene Croce's "Discussing the Undiscussable" appeared in *The New Yorker* at the turn of 1994–95; in it she declined to review Bill T. Jones's *Still/Here*, calling it "victim art".

## [[1999-06-15_Genius-in-Science-and-in-Art|Genius in Science and in Art]]
*1999-06-15 (estimated) · Paris, Beaux-Arts · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1997_2_p072.jpg|p. 72]]*

In science genius needs no masquerade one can say one is a scientist - it is a daily activity, a result of successful completion of a type of education. Too often I confound the word artist w/ the word genius where I should say an artist of genius. In fact only an artist of genius is interesting. An artist "tout court" is pathetic, no use, no interest. This for me is a fundamental problem in my manuvering in my small art world. And is a fundamental chauvanism I must get over or hide.
\_\_\_
My idea of genius is closer to the scientist's I think. Many people are working for a similar goal, sharing ideas & results after the fact, or through constant interaction one person is considered to be a genius by the others
The results, the advances are elsewhere.
