## [[1994-07-31_Arnheim-Art-and-Visual-Perception-Introduction|Arnheim: Art & Visual Perception Introduction (Page 92)]]
*1994-07-31 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · notebook p. 92 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p028.jpg|p. 28]]*

Notes from the **Introduction (pp. 1–6)** of Rudolf Arnheim's *Art and Visual Perception*:

- `"Our experiences and ideas tend to be common but not deep, or deep but not common"` — p. 1.
- Summarizes Arnheim's discussion of why artists and art teachers warn against excessive speech in the studio (experience of a Rembrandt painting cannot be fully reduced to explanation, though this limitation holds for all sensory experience, p. 2), how language names and conceptualizes distilled generalities rather than functioning as an alien medium, and why good art theory should still "smell of the studio" (p. 4).
- Defines **GESTALT** (German noun for shape or form) and its scientific lineage in **Max Wertheimer, Wolfgang Köhler, and Kurt Koffka** (p. 5), emphasizing that wholes cannot be attained by accreting isolated parts—an artistic sense of integrated structure that also guides reality.
- Closes on pp. 6: every element's appearance depends on its place and function in an overall pattern; seeing requires an interplay between object properties and observer (`"images of reality can be valid even though far removed from 'realistic' semblance"`); art is concrete and theory has no excuse for confusing anyone who wants to know more about it.

##### Summary of Arnheim Excerpts (pp. 17, 37, 40, 44)
- **p. 17 (Perceived image as the artwork):** Notes Arnheim's insistence that the perceived image—not physical pigment—is the work of art: perceptual forces pulling an unbalanced disk `[center-square sketch]` are artistically real even if physically "illusory."
- **p. 37 (Critique of Freud's death instinct & economy principle):** Summarizes Arnheim's rejection of Freud's claim that organisms strive toward inorganic rest—arguing instead that healthy human life fulfills itself in doing, moving, growing, and creating, waging an uphill battle against entropy.
  - Marco's marginal additions inside brackets:
    > `[Freud] assigned a fundamental role to the "death instinct,"... Man is lazy by nature [some men maybe. But maybe the most active find that life is easiest in activity. It is much easier for the marathoner to run 25 miles if his car breaks down, than to sit by the road and try to flag someone down]`
    > `[grass and trees are the only things which keep the rocks and dirt from falling off a mountain. They have to fight against the wind, sun, rain, melting snow & freezing ice which all loosen the rocks & dirt & send them down, closer to the center of gravity of the planet.]` — p. 37.
- **p. 40 (Music & Image — composition):** Compares the musical scale and framed visual composition in both combining rising intensity from base to peak with symmetry returning to a base/rest.
- **p. 44 (Gesture & caricature):** Summarizes Arnheim on seeing essential structural features (blueness of sky, curve of swan's neck, simple face strokes, Köhler's chimpanzees startled by button eyes, caricature catching a person's likeness through few lines). Closes with Marco's added note:
  > `- making art is the double process of seeing; then of making others see.` — p. 44.

## [[1994-08-03_Arnheim-on-Overall-Structure-and-Form-Meaning|Arnheim: Overall Structure, Simplicity & 'A Whole Maintains Itself' (Pages 101–101v)]]
*1994-08-03 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · notebook p. 101 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p041.jpg|p. 41]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p042.jpg|p. 42]]*

Notes from *Art & Visual Perception* **pp. 44, 45, 62, 67–68** alongside Marco's self-reflection on reading and writing:

##### Summary of Arnheim Excerpts
- **pp. 44–45 (*Detail*):** Summarizes anthropologists' finding that people unfamiliar with photography struggle to read "realistic" photos that Western viewers have learned to decipher, and that overall structural features (such as triangularity) are primary perceptual data rather than late abstractions.
- **p. 62 (*Simplicity — form & meaning in image & language*):** Summarizes Arnheim's argument that every artwork conveys meaning about existence (like a teapot interpreting its function to the eye), so simplicity depends on matching visual/verbal structure to intended thought—short sentences do not guarantee simplicity if form conflicts with meaning.
- **pp. 67–68 (*A Whole Maintains Itself | GESTALT*):** Summarizes how elements in a visual field depend on total context yet are selectively affected; contrasts physical wholes (water vs. rock, Michelangelo's *Madonna* marble unaffected physically by damage) with perceptual interactions in the nervous system (greenish tint of a pale face beside a red dress), citing architect **Eduardo Torroja** on a straight tie-bar curving optically against an arch or rectangle inside an ogive.

##### Marco's Reflection on Reading Arnheim vs. New Critics
> `[The new critics objection to quoting is a concern for the text as a gestalt, whereas it is less of one that an image or an object. One does not always readily comprehend or concieve the major structures of a text. One has to read one word at a time. The structure can help to bring one to an understanding at a certain point. Arnheim himself for all the attempts he has made to structure his work cannot concieve of it as a gestalt. He makes several points a paragraph. I find that the realizations I have made defy the structure of the whole, and that I have liked the process of returning to copy parts I have underlined more than I actually liked reading the chapters. The quotes make almost more sense or resonate more clearly when they are freed from the constraints and confusion of the many various thoughts in this text. I think that I think & write similarly to Arnheim, I have a lot of problems seeing the whole]`

## [[1994-08-03_Arnheim-Form-as-Visible-Shape-of-Content|Arnheim: Form as Content, Illusion, Equivalence & Spatial Images (Pages 102–102v)]]
*1994-08-03 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · notebook p. 102 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p043.jpg|p. 43]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p044.jpg|p. 44]]*

Notes from *Art & Visual Perception* **pp. 80, 96, 98, 105, 107, 130, 132** paired with Marco's annotations and spatial installation thought:

##### Summary of Arnheim Excerpts (pp. 80–132)
- **p. 80 (Single vs. narrative images):** Contrasts a film man pushing through a crowd vs. a still photograph where the artist must rupture still verticals visually: `"Still verticals ruptured by triangular shape?..."`
- **p. 96 (`"Form is the visible shape of content"` — Ben Shahn):** Summarizes how external shape, color, and behavior express inner nature (tea cup vs. knife; rabbit), Wittgenstein's drawn triangle seen as geometric figure, mountain, wedge, or pointer, and marble sculpture referencing living forms rather than stone. Includes Marco's contemporary addition:
  > `[Modern sculpture is more aware that the material speaks about the object — Ron Jones' sculpture made out of wood which it is illegal to export from South Africa, about S. Africa. Sand from..., each w/ its own colors etc.]`
- **Critique of "false naturalness" (pp. 96–97):** Notes Dürer's rabbit, Classic Maya stylized imagery, and Goethe's remark about transforming artifice into nature (`[Goethe's quote seems to sound like Zeuxsis or Giotto comments... I think I see what Arnheim is getting at b/c of the Maya reference above, but the Goethe quote is anything but clear.]`), plus Marco questioning Arnheim's wording on why a "draftsman" depicts what he knows (`Arny often falls into language traps... what are these rules?`).
- **pp. 98, 105, 107, 130, 132 (Projection, Equivalence & Rotating Shadows):** Summarizes that image-making renders an observed equivalent within a medium's properties rather than mechanical optical projection (`[à la grid sketch]`), explains perceptual constancy vs. retinal amoebic shifts (and Marco's note: `[This means that when a person sees a rotating shadow on a flat sheet he very well may see the precept conceived by the brain in 3D...]`), contrasts mobile film/stage sequences against frozen painting (`p. 105`, Marco noting: `again Arnheim's language becomes troublesome. I think it might serve me best to take ideas from him w/out quoting.`), notes `2D = translation of 3D p. 107`, merging partial impressions (`p. 130`), and modern artists recovering the innocent eye against fleeting glimpses (`p. 132`).

##### Marco's Closing Question (p. 102v)
- `- I return to my idea of creating a place by a bunch of images hung in spacial relation to each other which repeats the spacial relation the objects represented had to each other. Is this taking art forward or backwards?`

## [[1994-08-04_Giotto-Picasso-on-Resemblance-and-Drawing-a-Circle|Giotto, The Ambassadors & Picasso on Drawing a Perfect Circle]]
*1994-08-04 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p046.jpg|p. 46]]*

Observations from Boccaccio, perspective viewing angles, and Arnheim (**pp. 137–138**, leading directly into page 104):

- `[Boccacio's Decameron(e) speaks of Giotto - highly stylized = super reality -> like Zeuxis story of grapes in Pliny.]`
- Reflects on how a 2D painting hanging on a wall among 3D objects depicts 3D space yet remains flat—even viewed from sharp angles, the rectangle warps (`[4 trapezoid/parallelogram sketches]`) without forming composite spatial depth: `Take photographs of paintings from sharp angles to get warped images — like "Ambassadors"`.
- **Arnheim, p. 137 (cultural style & illusion):** Summarizes Arnheim's note that actual illusions are rare mainly because each culture perceives its own familiar style of representation directly as a faithful reproduction of the object.
- **Picasso (1966 / p. 138):**
  - Notes Picasso's 1966 remark that he aims at resemblance—observing nature while never confusing it with painting, since nature is translatable into painting only *"by signs."*
  - Notes Picasso's advice on striving for perfection: try to draw a perfect circle and the involuntary flaw reveals your personality, whereas trying deliberately to draw an imperfect, personal circle bungles the whole thing (`- p. 138`). *(Leads directly to Arnheim p. 138 on originality vs. personal style on notebook page 104.)*
