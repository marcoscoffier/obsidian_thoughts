## [[1994-07-26_Hulls-Knife-Edge-Between-Artist-and-Nature|Hull's Knife Edge Between the Painter and Nature]]
*1994-07-26 · Yale, New Haven · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p004.jpg|p. 4]]*

Hull mentioned in class yesterday—about having a relationship w/ nature—no relationship is interesting if one party always wins—the relationship will either end altogether or be terribly unhappy.

The relationship of a painter w/ nature is that between that which is going on in his head—gestures, expression & that which he gathers from nature. If nature always wins (trompe l'oeil [or] other forms of direct copying)—then the work gets boring—as does Abstract expressionism or another form which is all about the artist. I like to try to walk this knife edge, mixing the gesture, small recognitions or reminiscences w/ a careful comparison to what I see before me.

I'm not quite sure what I am saying, my argument kind of unraveled as I wrote it down & I feel that I am left w/ a handful of string.

## [[1994-08-03_Lake-Annecy-Sparkling-Water-and-Backlit-Birds|Sitting on a Bench at Lake Annecy: Sparkling Water & Birds (Page 101)]]
*1994-08-03 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · notebook p. 101 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p041.jpg|p. 41]]*

I sat on a bench in Annecy looking at the lake from under the shade of a large tree, finally I could content myself after a long day of being w/ a group who flirted w/ experience rather than partake in it. The sun was in such a position that I could not see it, but its presence was easily percieved by the glowing outlines of some branches and leaves above me and by the flashes of light, with the sparkling feel of a discoteche casion [casino?] or christmas tree, off the ever changing water, which I thought looked like tight Saran wrap, then turmoiled mercury — then nothing so specific or so muscular. The changing nature of the waves affected the flashing sun on its surface from the frenetic excitement of a disco to erratic twinkling of an elderly matron's sequined robe. The birds would react to the changes of the wind, waves and the sun which all changed each other. When the backlit birds would land, their initial impact w/ the water would be followed by a sparkling ring of sun.

> *(Right margin, sideways):* `this is an effect to be captured w/ a brush or a camera.`

The artist must scorn all judgement that is not based on an intelligent observation of character.
