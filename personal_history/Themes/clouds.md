## [[1994-07-29_Flying-Over-Clouds-Diebenkorn-Turrell-and-Earth-Flatness|Flying Washington–NY: Clouds, Diebenkorn, Turrell & Earth's Flatness (Page 88)]]
*1994-07-29 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · notebook p. 88 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p019.jpg|p. 19]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p020.jpg|p. 20]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p021.jpg|p. 21]]*

Flying from Washington to NY I had a window seat. It had been storming and there were many clouds of every shape and kind and color in the sky. It was really quite beautiful. The plane was flying inside these strips. There were thin black strips (pollution?—I thought they were kind of beautiful), puffy cottonny, mountainous ones and large flat scrim like ones. The earth was mostly a dark kind of greyed out purple color because for the most part someone from the ground would see complete cloud cover, he could not see the differentiation that we could from our privaledged, airborne position. We would however often see breaks in the clouds above us and the blue sunny sky (Is it comforting to think that no matter how bad the weather, it is always a sunny day if you could just fly high enough?)

I remember Hollander mentioning that he was surprised that painters or artists had not reacted to views or visual stimulation which has been made possible by technological advances which allow humans all sorts of different views of the world. I am happy to mention that two of the artists about whom I have been most interested in the recent past, Diebenkorn and Turrell, both claim the visual experience of flying as a direct influence to their work. Diebenkorn vividly remembered his first plane flight, from New Mexico to the Bay Area (I believe) and the evidence can be seen in some of the later Berkley abstractions (50's before he switched to figuration) like the yellow one with black lines which Katherine copied. Turrell openly claims that his job as a small airplane pilot which has given him frequent views of the split heaven and earth is a direct influence to his pieces. He also mentions the multiple colors of the changing sunlight at twilight. He flew up and down the flat—and I think this is important—regions of Eastern California & the West to search for Roden Crater—he seeks to make a sculpture which directly confronts the viewer with a feeling of ½ heavens ½ earth.

> *[Diagram, top of p. 88]:* Cross-section of `Crater ->` bowl with a tiny standing viewer at the bottom and dotted sightlines forming a huge circle with the caption: `optical effect the sky "curves" creates a sphere w/ the earth. new horizon`.

I think it may be important that Diebenkorn doesn't admit to being openly influenced by anything `[above crossed-out: I thought of Diebenkorn's abstractions which critics have connected to what]`. He even would attack paintings, and "ruin" them to try to remove elements of figuration or representation which had "appeared" in them. Eventually, he stopped ruining pictures like that and would leave these elements as in *"Motorcycle Crash"* and *"The Archer"*. Diebenkorn doesn't want to think consciously about what goes into his abstractions and why. It is the critics who see evidences of the Ocean Park Architecture and landscape in the Ocean Park Series. I don't know if Diebenkorn is being completely honest about not thinking but I don't think one can completely eliminate the evidence of previous visual stimulae, a painter "names" visually even if he does not "name" things verbally. `[p. 40 *] [see the Wilson quote about memory & stars]`

Flying the other day, I was thinking about how flat the landscape really is, human beings can't do anything yet to affect this phenomena (word choice?—I don't have a definite definition in mind, though I know a thorough definition would be several pages long.) This flatness is similar to a painting's flatness—there is only so thick a painting can get—Frank Stella's (3ft?), Anselm Keiffer (1.5ft?) and likewise for the landscape, the Himalayan mountain range is the largest rupture in this flatness, 28,000m = 28km how does this compare to 40,000 km (diameter or circumference) of the earth. The earth is flatter than the paintings I have mentioned. Thus the earth's surface as seen from the sky could be the perfect subject matter for an abstract painting and the obvious reason why someone like Diebenkorn, who is somehow more true to his own visual experience than other painters who have painted abstractly, comes to make paintings reminiscent of such a landscape, which he admits having been excited by at the time. He cannot shake himself from the visual baggage which he is carrying and representing it. He is representing an image which exists only in his head, but I believe he is representing it & it does exist. The same sort of thing happens w/ Rothko over Still or Newman. People claim to see the sunset or the horizon or the sky in his works. He aims at a specific visual effect, his paint colors & tones are so carefully balanced. When he achieves the effect he desires that could very well be when the painting has matched an image he has, no matter how vague, of a large visual effect like a sunset, or the horizon.

## [[1994-07-30_Clouds-Screens-Colored-Filters-and-Shadow-Rooms|Cloud Spaces, Screens & Colored Filter Shadow Room]]
*1994-07-30 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p021.jpg|p. 21]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p022.jpg|p. 22]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p023.jpg|p. 23]]*

I was excited by the feeling I got in the plane when we were inside the cloud layers. I thought right away of ways I could recreate such a feeling in a sculpted space.

My first idea was rather basic: room full of cloud like materials which the viewer can pass through. *[Sketch of cube room]* I would especially like the effect of passing through a cloud, not under, over or around a cotton representation of one—that is a marvelous feature of clouds, that one can never experience their surface, it does not exist up close, only from afar. Even if a parachuter jumped from the plane onto the soft white billowing surface he would go right through and never be quite sure at what point he passed through what he had thought was a surface. If someone in a plane took a movie of his jump they could approximate maybe even name a point to a certain degree of accuracy at which he actually penetrated that surface, but everything in which I am interested lies in that degree of accuracy.

**My second idea (warmer):**
When you look at it from afar, it looks like a solid wall — but when you approach it you realize that it is actually possible to pass right through.
> *[Diagrams: `Front` square view of wave/horizon strips -> `Side` staggered vertical hanging strips; `I could maybe achieve such an effect with glass.` -> `Front` horizon panel -> angled layered glass partitions & snaking visitor path diagram; `more layers would probably be needed to have this effect at all`]*

- What about the wood slats?... `<- SCREENS!` — ocean waves.
- This is about the excitement I had about layers, textures on top *[crosshatch sketch]* — ocean waves w/ wind, earth layers evident in mountains etc & even about Erica's photographs of the chalk boards which have their own kind of history of layered sediment (marks representing thought or all kinds of diagrams really—which stimulate thought?)
- The beginning of *Le Premier Homme*.

I remember when I was much younger, erasing all the smudges and stray marks off of a drawing I had done & not liking it anymore, it became stale to me. I then remember making drawing where I would intentionally smudge things and leave stray marks because I wanted it to have the same feel as the drawing I had ruined by cleaning up. I didn't like this second drawing either because the smudges etc. were contrived & didn't add to the life of the drawing (`; (these are words` `[unclear/edge clipped] now I wouldn't have used them`).

##### Translucent Slats, RGB Filters & the Black Wall
The shadows would multiply & get complicated in the crossings of slated planes, it could become dense enough that it would be impossible to see through, or completely dark in the back from a lighted front.

Layers of plastic of different colors could create complete blackness. It would require a passage through these films to realize that the piece was made up of many colored translucent sheets. I just screwed [my] red & green filters together which created a beautiful brown (warm)—I would need blue to make it black (or grey). I could not tell, once screwed together which side was red & which green. I took them apart and held them in line about an inch apart so that I saw only brown through one eye, and noticed that the light, which was on an angle cast the shadows of green & red of the individual filters — might be possible to make a thick frame around each one which would make it impossible to see the color of the gels except by the shadows that would be cast — I might build a special shadow capturer.
> *[Diagram: `light ->` shining at an angle across four framed colored sheets (`looks black`) casting separated `rainbow shadows` below.]*

- w/ my room w/ lattice layers, shall I paint the back wall black? Or... just got a great idea, viewer enters the dark area sees black wall but as he works his way through to the front, it is illuminated by a large window, and the visual effect from the beginning is no longer evident, it has been made evident.

## [[1995-07-01_On-the-Plane-Home-from-Jackson|On the Plane Home from Jackson]]
*1995-07-01 (estimated) · DC, Post-College · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1995_p084.jpg|p. 84]]*

On the plane home from Jackson
I want to
fall through the thick coils of air
wipping around my flesh
tugging at my hairs
to land in the soft white silence
of a cloud.

There I could
tumble and scramble
through its billowy whiteness
climbing my way through
the bottomless formlessness
In sun and shadow, wind and silence
Alone, tired and happy.

I woke up very early this saturday morning. I heard some noises I guess and thought some one was trying to steal our bikes. The house makes so much noise when there is no other noise, the doors squeak the floor ~~squeaks~~ creaks. Even when I am not moving in the cold outside looking up at ~~[?]~~ the stars through a crack of sky between the buildings I can hear the tings of metal expanding as the morning warms it slightly and of course I can hear the whole building sleeping.
Some of the magic of clouds is that they make no noise even when moving very swiftly.
