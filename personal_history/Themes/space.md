## [[1994-07-13_Ways-to-Make-a-Space-Feel-Huge|Ways to Make a Space Feel Huge]]
*1994-07-13 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 61 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p104.jpg|p. 104]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p105.jpg|p. 105]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p106.jpg|p. 106]]*

* **Cut off the viewer's body:** Just the head [visible]—smaller makes the space look bigger.
* **Skylights & Illusion of Space:** Putting a window high up near the ceiling in my room, painting the sky on the wall behind the window.
* **Water-Plane Effect:** Like walking on water in I.M. Pei's Louvre fountains—if you look across, you can't see the grooves, it looks like a flat plane, yet people's heads are passing through this plane.
* **Casement Window & Grid Depth:** A window with a grid where, rather than erase an inch of painting everywhere the lattice covers it, I would split the painting—emulating a stereoscopic effect. Because with both eyes you can see around a bar or a pole when you focus beyond it, each eye sees a little on each side and the pole effectively vanishes.
* **Concealed Fluorescent Mirror:** Put a mirror in front of a fluorescent light that can't be seen; the light comes around the mirror, and the mirror looks suspended in mid-air.

Michel Collot Du sens de l'espace à l'espace du sens
