## [[1994-08-20_Draft-Letter-to-Katherine|Draft Letter to Katherine: Vocation & Companionship]]
*1994-08-20 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · notebook p. 32 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p054.jpg|p. 54]]*

My language is so tense (my handwriting so small). I feel that things changed so much, I'm not sure how to address you. What are you thinking, feeling? What will your first letter to me be like?

I'm feeling very much out on my own—detached from home, and my friends so far, and from you. I'm trying to figure myself out. What do I want to do? Do I gear myself up for some kind of profession? Further studying? A life of academia? What about art? Am I more of an artist at heart? If so, more conceptual? Emotional? A painter? I can't really start planning any life till I know some kind of answer to these questions.

Should I include you in some sort of plan? It seems that if we wanted to see each other for anything more than a short period—which can't be satisfying in the long term, and I feel that is a weak situation for a relationship, though it is well praised by romance novels—on the other hand, I think that I will think of you in many situations in my life even if we are not together.

Which brings me to my next thought: I think that what we began to cultivate (our relationship) was really incredible. I've never felt so natural and empowered. You became a true companion. I'm not trying to say that we should be friends—I loved sharing everything with you: the double sleeping bags and waking up late in the woods, Shalimar, and several nights at Daria's. The essence of our relationship, I think, is a similarity of spirit and thought.

## [[1995-02-21_The-Stamp-on-My-Last-Letter-to-Katherine|The Stamp on My Last Letter to Katherine]]
*1995-02-21 · Final Semester Yale 1995 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1995_p039.jpg|p. 39]]*

2/21 Tues.
I can still taste the stamp I put on my last letter to katherine.
I sat and stared at it against the beige formica of the table in CCL. The image on the stamp glowing. The other two destined to remain un-used until they are lost are still neat & crisp. I just bought them the other day - the first time I made the decision to buy a bunch at once rather than always have to wait in line when I wanted to send her a letter. My life changes too much ever to make plans for the future, Even buying stamps.
The letter sits here before me with ~~[?]~~ memorized address ~~[?]~~ about to embark on a journey w/ the four other letters on their way to her. They're all gonna get there in a bunch. Each one so different from the other, a document of my radically shifting feelings 'till this last one ~~[?] to tell her~~ which contains ~~[?]~~ ~~[?]~~ my breakdown and the subsequent cool acceptance. I do feel comfortable and good. My ego is as strong as hers. She can't get me down. She can't always deny what she let herself feel for me. I don't want <u>her</u> reticent love anymore.
`[at the foot of the page: two unused 40¢ US Airmail stamps, "William T. Piper, Aviation Pioneer", pasted in]`

> [!note]- Transcription notes
> The two stamps pasted at the bottom of the page are probably "the other two destined to remain un-used". CCL is Yale's Cross Campus Library. 21 February 1995 was a Tuesday.

katherine ~~[?]~~ didn't fall in love w/ the actual physical me, only the poetic potential we shared - Griffin & Sabine or Exupéry's Lettres à ~~[?]~~ l'amie inventée. It is not the me physical me she loved - maybe why I felt those two parts of myself so seperate in my relationship with her.
`[an arrow from the circled "katherine":]` → I see her name on the page & I have no idea who or what it refers to for me - I wrote her letters & letters we loved on paper through each other's handwriting but I don't know if we ever actually felt it when we were together - even if we had, its all a memory now.

> Undated; it follows the 2/27 entries. *Griffin & Sabine* (1991) is Nick Bantock's novel told in letters. Saint-Exupéry's *Lettres à l'amie inventée* is a collection of his letters to a young woman he never met.
