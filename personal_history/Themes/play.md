## [[1994-07-30_Bateson-Theory-of-Play-and-Fantasy|Notes on Semiotics: Bateson's Theory of Play & Fantasy (Page 90)]]
*1994-07-30 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · notebook p. 90 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p024.jpg|p. 24]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p025.jpg|p. 25]]*

**Notes on Semiotics: Bateson, *Theory of Play & Fantasy***

##### Summary of Bateson Excerpts
- Contrasts involuntary animal mood-signs (sexual odor) with human cosmetics and perfumes worn voluntarily, which nevertheless produce automatic/auto-suggestive effects even on the wearer; links this evolutionary threshold—eating of the Tree of Knowledge to discover that *signals are signals*—to human language, empathy, identification, and multiple levels of abstraction.
- Outlines **metalinguistic** messages (about language, e.g. `"the word 'cat' has no fur and cannot scratch"`) vs. **metacommunicative** messages (about the speakers' relationship, e.g. `"This is play"`, mostly implicit), where `"This is play"` expands to the paradox that these actions do not denote what they would denote.
- Notes that inside a dream, the dreamer lacks the concept `"untrue"` and cannot normally frame the dream, while schizophrenic `"word salad"` omits the `"as if"` frame-setting message, acting out metaphors literally.
