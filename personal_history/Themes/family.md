## [[1994-06-15_Letter-to-Edo-Part-1-Shared-Experience|Letter to Edo (Part 1 — Shared Experience)]]
*1994-06-15 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 18 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p027.jpg|p. 27]]*

1) I'm not talking for Mom, or using Mom's words. Mom & I are very similar, her outlook is in many respects very similar to my own.
- You have no idea how much you are hurting her.
- I hear so much about you secondhand. It is very hard not to have an intimate relationship w/ your own brother. I feel you don't tell me things, for the same reason you don't tell Mom things.
- I understand the feeling that you want to withdraw from society & do your own thing, like those guys in the cabin in Waterford—like [an] acid trip—this is for you & only you; it is not a shared experience. I don't know if you are any good at the guitar at all, I've never heard you play—only mess around.

You are so critical of everyone, I can only imagine how critical you are of yourself. I believe that one's *[crossed out: your]* life doesn't really mean anything unless one has *[crossed out: you have]* shared it with others—the more the better.

- I don't buy this "can't grow under the shade of an oak" shit—the you and Richie lame-out. Whatever I have done, it's no big wup (deal); hundreds of thousands of people have done more, better. The fact is that the world is so absolutely full of fascinating things to learn & experience & then pass on that there can never be enough people to do it. You are only on this earth for so many years. Make the most of it. Take everything that comes your way & embrace it, learn from it, let it change you, don't settle on any revelation—it will get boring. Acid is not some hidden mystery, which will revolutionize the world if people would only try it.

## [[1994-07-27_Hull-Bonding-With-His-Son-Through-Drawing|Hull & His Son Drawing as Equals, Plus Sky Painting Tips (Page 81)]]
*1994-07-27 · Yale, New Haven · notebook p. 81 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p007.jpg|p. 7]]*

`*` Before coming over Hull went by the comic store to buy his kid some comics that he could read in the car on the way to Baltimore: *"We bond through drawing"* Hull said, *"He loves drawing super-heroes (Thats all I drew when I was young - Hull)"*. He showed me a small sketch book he had taken to the ballgame with his son. *"He could never sit through a whole game, he doesn't understand it, but if we're drawing he'll stay 3 hrs."* In the book the pages alternated Dad & son. *"With drawing we can be equals, I'm not teaching him anything. We're both artists."* A strange comment coming from a Yale professor. I know he's dying to have his son become an artist which was hard for him & will be for me (if I decide to do it) because it was so foreign to what our parents did. It will be absolutely natural for Hull's son — both his parents are artists, And he will have been drawing the way I would listen to music w/ my mom, he's not even 5 yet. Those memories from before real memory begins are so powerful.

Before I forget, Hull had two tips about painting large flat planes: mix 3 big pots up first of maybe 3 sky colors, to have enough to cover whole sky & maybe I should paint those areas w/ shorter strokes in the quick paintings, I pick up my brush, whereas the longer ones are constantly scumbled. I have to find if the high detail multiple reflections is really for me or something more about the gesture — maybe both?

## [[1994-07-29_French-Dead-Metaphors-Pain-de-Sucre-and-Allonge|French Dead Metaphors: Un Pain de Sucre & Un Weekend Allongé (Page 89)]]
*1994-07-29 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · notebook p. 89 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p018.jpg|p. 18]]*

I was thinking about French metaphors (or dead metaphors really) today in the car listening to my dad speak.

At breakfast before leaving: *Un pain de sucre*—a bread of sugar makes absolutely no sense in english but it is the dead figure in French for a sugar cube.

*Un weekend allongé*: My first thought was to a lying down weekend which is the primary meaning (or at least the meaning I first equate to *allonger*) but then I thought of an extended weekend. Extend can also mean to lie down in the reflexive to extend one's self or body but this is a secondary meaning. A dictionary would definitely help this thought.

## [[1994-07-31_Mom-Michael-Heizer-and-Rhodes-Empowerment|Mom on Michael Heizer, Rhodes Questions & Super Empowerment (Page 91)]]
*1994-07-31 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · notebook p. 91 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p026.jpg|p. 26]]*

Mom told me today that Michael Heizer married one of her friends from college, and that he was an ass; she only met him a few times but thought he was so pretentious. I didn't really doubt that she would agree w/ my dislikes.

I've talked to her a lot today & she really gives me quite an ego boost, even about the sample questions given to Rhodes scholars which had made me so uncomfortable when I first read them. I feel so much stronger & better about what I am doing, knowing that by following my feelings and instincts (passions) I can't really do wrong — Super empowerment!

## [[1994-08-01_Wiping-Away-Teenage-Fog-and-Brigittes-Anger|Morning Coffee Jitters, Self-Clarity & Brigitte's Anger (8/1)]]
*1994-08-01 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p038.jpg|p. 38]]*

**8/1** I have that jittery early in the morning feeling or is it that early in the morning jittery coffee feeling or the coffee early in the morning jittery feeling like I'm going to need a nap later but can't sleep now because I feel so jittery.

Mr. Extrasiuz making a lip flapping criket whistle for which he has to lick his lips which contorts his whole face and he wiggles his ears. *troooo. trooo. trenenenenp* *tren en en cup*

It feels really good to find myself. I feel like I've wiped away the fog of teenage confusion and fads and can see or feel clearly who I am. I know what I think of my cousin André refering to "le sex faible" and can feel Brigitte's anger, numbed by years of acceptance when André or my father speak of Italian women or an ex-movie star that who is now in a wheel chair. I imagine it must have been quite empowering for Brigitte to be one of four sisters, but each of the men the four sisters married finds their empowerment amusing, a woman's game. Papa makes me sick when he said *"Tu sais, les filles sont meilleures cette année, tu sais celles qu'on voit dans la rue."* *"Surtout les Italiennes?"* interjected Brigitte, *"Oui plutôt"...*
