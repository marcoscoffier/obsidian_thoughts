> [!note]- Transcription notes
> Undated. Notes from a talk by Bernice Rose, the MoMA drawings curator, on Joseph Beuys's drawings. "Iodine" is a reading; Beuys's materials were usually felt, fat, wax and honey.

## [[1998-12-15_Dubuffet-on-Childrens-Drawings-Graffiti-and-Invention|Dubuffet on Children's Drawings, Graffiti and Invention]]
*1998-12-15 (estimated) · Paris, Beaux-Arts · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1997_2_p032.jpg|p. 32]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1997_2_p033.jpg|p. 33]]*

<u>Dubuffet</u>. <u>Children</u>
`[a bracket in the margin joins this paragraph to the next, with an arrow from the title]`
"I don't like children. They don't really seem to exist they are like mirrors, small imitators. They are all monkeys, imitating whatever you do. One child copies the art of the other. I've seen exhibitions of children's drawings, especially in England, in which each school is recognisable; Matisse in one, Chagall in another.
In 1942 I was looking at the drawings of children alot. I was influenced by them, now less so. You see, I realized that children have an appetite for the civilization of adults. They want to be adults. Their art comes, in time, to express a cultural transcription of the word: a house, a tree, a sun, a dog. I feel, their art depicts <u>language</u>, the language of their elementary vocabulary, and not what they are really concerned with. They aren't actually interested in houses etc. The painting of children is completely "dictionary". p. 43 vol. IV

**Next page:**

<u>Graphitti</u>: In 1944 I was interested in old walls, and the drawings on them. But much of it is useless, mere copying. Graphitti is only of interest ~~when it is done~~ the first time it is done. For me, there is only one art; when you have invention! this is the real creativity, to recreate the world entirely with one's own hands, which Klee began.
<u>p. 55</u>
Art consists in constructing or inventing a mirror in which all the universe is reflected. An artist is a man who creates, or recreates, a parallel universe, who doesn't want an imposed universe inflicted upon him. He wants to do it himself, like a fish who excretes the water in which he swims. I believe that the creation of art is intimately linked w/ the spirit of revolt, a refusal to adopt things given by custom. That is ~~the~~ a true definition of insanity `[in red, with a bracket round the passage:]` not urbanism
\_\_\_
p57 There is nothing worse for art and creativity than the critical approach. Comparison is dangerous and destructive. Art is unique and not to be compared. If I'm looking at ~~the~~ Mont Blanc, I don't want to compare it to other mountains

> [!note]- Transcription notes
> Undated. A quotation from Jean Dubuffet, probably translated by Marco from his collected writings (*Prospectus et tous écrits suivants*, vol. IV); the opening quotation mark is not closed on the page. The red "not urbanism" beside the passage on revolt is a later comment.

## [[1998-12-15_Dewey-Stevens-and-Drawing-in-Perspective|Dewey, Stevens, and Drawing in Perspective]]
*1998-12-15 (estimated) · Paris, Beaux-Arts · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1997_2_p053.jpg|p. 53]]*

After a great read of Dewey's Art as Experience - I walked to the supermarket in the late evening summer sun - w/ parts of Wallace Steven's Ordinary evening in N. Haven in my mind - the sun - strong sun.
Steven's begins the poem very visually
I thought again of drawing - of the information you gain on your own visual system and of your place in the world when going through ~~the~~ a methodical representation of it.
I thought of the first users of perspective `[above:]` → imitation. who translated natural `[above:]` natural? space into the flat picture plane (there is a ~~strange~~ uncanny similarity btw the first perspective paintings & 3D computer animation
I thought how the act of drawing in perspective radically changes your view of your place in the world.
And I thought of all this learning about seeing & being that people have built up over the years & how this knowledge builds on thousands of peoples experience
`[a bracket and arrow in the margin:]` and how long it would take a machine to achieve these understandings
(see drawing in 10 pgs.)

> [!note]- Transcription notes
> Undated; a summer evening. John Dewey's *Art as Experience* (1934); Wallace Stevens's "An Ordinary Evening in New Haven", which Marco planned to put on his viewing stand in March 1995.
