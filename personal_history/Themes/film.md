## [[1995-01-05_Seeing-Red|Seeing "Red"]]

I just saw "Red". It was quite wonderful. Absolutely understated, but the time spent watching that movie slipped by like the time of a wonderful day when one is alone. Small coincidences or the most banal symbols like the color red - on the doors in the veterenarians office or on the wrapping paper of the 'Poire: ~~[?]~~ Valentine is so normal, the retired judge (Auguste Sr?) is too. The movie is understated enough to make its remarkable occurences ~~[?]~~ believable. ↳ make one smile, happy, in a way that is impossible to describe to anyone else. One feels like one has shared in a delightful secret - the best kind of understanding that is unexplainable. `[an arrow from "Poire:" leads to "↳ make one smile", the end of that sentence]`
I think of Chp. 5 of <u>Olysseus</u> the 'Wandering Rocks' episode with music as its art. where the paths of all the players especially the most insiginificant are mapped synonymously.

> [!note]- Transcription notes
> Undated; it comes after the 1/5/95 entry and before 1/6/95, so it takes the 1/5/95 date. "Red" is Kieślowski's *Trois couleurs : Rouge* (1994). The film's Auguste is a young judge whose life repeats the retired judge's, hence "(Auguste Sr?)". The joined-up "Valentine is so normal ... believable" sits between "Poire:" and the arrowed ending, so it reads as an aside. "Olysseus" is Joyce's *Ulysses*; "Wandering Rocks" is its tenth episode.
