## [[1998-12-15_Hong-Kong-River-and-the-Loneliness-of-Picture-Taking|Hong Kong River, and the Loneliness of Picture Taking]]
*1998-12-15 (estimated) · Paris, Beaux-Arts · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1997_2_p054.jpg|p. 54]]*

I recall more often than any other memory of my few days in Hongkong - a scene by a river between tall housing projects - but not crowded by them - many boys were fishing in this green seen filled cement trough of a river - I watched the fishing for ~~[?]~~ quite a while maybe 45 minutes but never took the picture - the scene wasn't dense enough for a photo.
\- like the rue des Ecoles when biking back from Bastille, like Connecticut avenue near `[above:]` the begining of the uptown, there is something too spread out about these places -
There is a deep loneliness in picture taking
\- Underlines perhaps how singular everyones view point really is - by showing us the singularity of the photographer's vision - his vision is condensed - b/c a photo condenses What was in front of his eyes becomes condensed & small what was behind becomes enourmous and empty. `[a bracket in the right margin, with:]` needs further explanation.
When I take a picture the whole 360° of spherical experience is around me - This is condensed into the visual part that fits into a picture. I take this picture and place it on a white wall w/ enourmous amounts of white space around it & behind me when I look at it on this wall. The viewer steps into the photographer's loneliness. when he moves before the image
