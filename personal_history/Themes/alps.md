## [[1994-07-31_Unedited-Hiking-Video-and-Butors-Chien-Roi|Unedited Hiking Video Sculpture & Butor's Chien Roi (Page 91)]]
*1994-07-31 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · notebook p. 91 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p026.jpg|p. 26]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p027.jpg|p. 27]]*

As I was hiking up to make my first mountain painting (which needs help, but is a better start than I expected) I came up w/ another sculpture idea.

An unedited video tape of my stream of conscience while hiking — the breathing, the footsteps. The stream of thoughts that flew through my head as I actually pretended to be doing this was amazing. First (though I didn't think of it first) I would show the film in a special space which would be set up in such a way that the viewer would feel like they were in my body. Paint large black (weight) heavy shape behind — my backpack. People would stream in & out, but who would stay? I love the recap felt when viewing ski training films, what order people went, who fell when, what inadvertent things did the instructor say when filming? — I was thinking of borrowing Authier's camera, would he let me? could I rent one?

I want the tape to be uncut — a performance by me even slips out of natural feeling would be recorded & thus be natural. I would have to include comments about what I was doing, how it is hard to walk w/ camera on my eye. Start looking into a mirror? or take photo of me w/ camera? No better to let viewer form own; then the viewer will probably identify w/ it more. Would I be as brilliant as I was today? would I get self-conscious even w/ the camera? — Probably. I want to try to do this — here.

I made a connection to the new critics not wanting to quote or paraphrase, thus I would not edit, yet this small amount of time in comparison to my life is nothing more than a quote of my thoughts during that moment, thus I wouldn't try to fill in a viewer as to what had been going on in my head before. If they had questions they would have to ask me (I would be present at the showing — where I was thinking in the center of the room to get the full effect? or not present? or non-chalant mingling?) Basically, I guess I would explain as much as I do in here in this journal. What do I explain? Is there a pattern? Someone else would have to find that out, or me in a couple years.

---

I was reading Butor's *Chien Roi* & thought of the careful placement of images in the text. The placement of an image is important b/c once one has seen it, it remains in the mind & affects the rest of the reading. Thus, when he put the image of "Tunnel" after the poem, he is making us very aware of this fact, we see tunnel w/ the baggage of his poem rather than read "Tunnel" w/ the baggage of the image. *see 28b.*

## [[1994-07-31_Alps-Space-Stereoscopic-Boxes-and-Sound-Bike-Room|Alps Space, Stereoscopic Boxes & Sound-Bike Octagon Room (Page 93)]]
*1994-07-31 · Summer 1994, Travel and Alps · notebook p. 93 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p029.jpg|p. 29]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p030.jpg|p. 30]]*

**Visual experience in Literature beyond the ekphrastic.**

A 2D picture traps space w/in it. the viewer is kept from experiencing the space in which the photographer, or painter, was standing by the edges of his image. The edges, the frame defines it as an image but the viewer cannot fly through the space on that flat plane; He is constantly grounded by the frame. It is impossible for me to convey my feeling about the Alps on paper with photo emulsion, pencil or paint b/c that experience has everything to do with the feeling of space I experience in the mountains.

`((` Writers can tap into this space b/c their medium is narrative. In order to experience space as space, one has to move through it. The visual experience of a flat image is static in time & space. One cannot move through it, into it, but only around it. It is frozen in time.

I did a sculpture project about stereoscopic vision last semester and the culmination of a series of investigations was a set of boxes where I cut up "Stereoscopic" photographs — I had taken 2 views of a still-life, right eye/left eye, I xeroxed one onto acetate on a color copier, then pasted the clear one over its partner. — I cut these photographs by object and placed them in a box at the relative distances these objects had had in the original still life. *[Cube box sketch with layered cutout planes inside]* I was left w/ a lot of empty space btw. these slices of picture. There was no way of looking at the box so that the pictures would come back together as one.

Maybe it would be possible to make such a box, the viewer would have to be directed towards the particular initial viewing spot — sees flat image. Then, when he approaches it, he realizes that they are forced into space.
