## [[1994-07-28_Train-Station-Yale-Bike-Guilt-and-Going-to-France|Train Station Packing, Yale/Bike Guilt & Going to France (Page 86)]]
*1994-07-28 · notebook p. 86 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p015.jpg|p. 15]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p016.jpg|p. 16]]*

I sat in the train station today, and was surrounded by my stuff—what I am going to take to France—I used to consider it normal, it was not something I would hide from people. I have been away from that person who goes to France for awhile. Trying to save money in the apartment, saving, paring down to my mobile painting unit & what I need to exercise, using the "Free" library, feeling bad that my appearance might be alienating—that people would look at me and think `[Yale Student]` `[Rich White boy]`. I even told the two kids who come to watch me paint in Fairhaven that I went to Yale. Feeling guilty about my bike everywhere I go feeling that it's being looked at `´EXPENSIVE`. I feel the guilt as I lock up both wheels and the frame & take the seat inside w/ me because I think all the eyes that are watching it might be eyes in the dark bushes, or a gang of eyes eager to impress each other. But today I didn't care. "I'm going to France" I thought when I saw the group of pre-teen kids wearing expensive shoes and color matched clothing. "I'm going to France" and "I've gone every summer".

I have to get over my guilt riddenness & not become callous at the same time—this is probably impossible. No man could translate *"L'Histoire d'O"* he would have been too guilty to get across the fantasy's that Dominique Aubé [Aury] had had. Fantasies she claims are her own.—A woman can be free to share these enslavement fantasy's but *(bottom-left margin p. 86)* a man cannot openly (comfortably) fantasize about being the master. It is easy to live in a culture, everything is defined for you but when you start to question that culture you are suddenly treading in the wake of all cultures, clinging on to the best bit of debris which happens to float by at that particular moment. How can your experience in this maelstrom ever be matched to someone else's. Can a cross cultural marriage exist? Does it make sense? Does every culture have marriage?

> *(Margin note, p. 86 left):* that I went to France every summer
- of course these changes are mostly superficial, what I need to excersize exceeds what most people need.
- it is very hard to turn back on technology once you have used it (this is what creates the communities)

The Library is "free" b/c my parents are going to pay $25,000 for my last year at Yale and therefore I'm technically still enrolled.

I would stay home & cook rather than go out to eat (dad is getting to me) but even at home I mentioned making something w/ Dijon mustard which I will always have around & the women at work commented as though I was some kind of gourmet or an esthete etc.

It is a strange feeling bringing these pages, conceived in the individual, creative freedom I felt in Summer New Haven, home & shortly to France. It's been a long year. The first one I ever would have wanted to skip out on going to France.

## [[1994-07-29_Lighthouse-Point-with-Amy-and-Literary-Sea-Memory|Pier at Lighthouse Point w/ Amy & Literary Sea Memory (Page 89)]]
*1994-07-29 · notebook p. 89 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p018.jpg|p. 18]]*

I was standing on the end of a Pier w/ Amy at Lighthouse Point. We were on large rectangular rocks which had obviously been placed there by men but long enough ago that they were covered in thick slimy green and any indentation was filled w/ life and shells. I was reminded of V. Woolf's *"Kew Gardens"*. Some children playing in such a puddle could enlarge it until it became as big as the famous English gardens. I am always reminded of a picture in *Outside* magazine (I should buy a macro adaptor-filter?)

We then got up and faced the wind on the sea. It was very hazy so the sun wasn't coming from any particular direction though it was strong and made us squint. We were talking about literature. "Have you read...?" "No, Have you read...?" All my perceptions of the sea have been formed by novels about a young man who goes off to sea—*Horatio Hornblower*, *Nostromo*—I know there are others but I can't remember their names. My image of the sea has been formed by these books. I've never spent much time near ports, weathered boards, barnacles, heavy ropes & pulleys, wet canvas. I know of these things only through an image I have created in conjunction with the books I have read. These books impregnate the image of any of these objects, say the barnacles on the rocks w/ Amy, with a memory which is not my own, but one in me which was created by these books.

## [[1994-07-29_Flying-Over-Clouds-Diebenkorn-Turrell-and-Earth-Flatness|Flying Washington–NY: Clouds, Diebenkorn, Turrell & Earth's Flatness (Page 88)]]
*1994-07-29 · notebook p. 88 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p019.jpg|p. 19]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p020.jpg|p. 20]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p021.jpg|p. 21]]*

Flying from Washington to NY I had a window seat. It had been storming and there were many clouds of every shape and kind and color in the sky. It was really quite beautiful. The plane was flying inside these strips. There were thin black strips (pollution?—I thought they were kind of beautiful), puffy cottonny, mountainous ones and large flat scrim like ones. The earth was mostly a dark kind of greyed out purple color because for the most part someone from the ground would see complete cloud cover, he could not see the differentiation that we could from our privaledged, airborne position. We would however often see breaks in the clouds above us and the blue sunny sky (Is it comforting to think that no matter how bad the weather, it is always a sunny day if you could just fly high enough?)

I remember Hollander mentioning that he was surprised that painters or artists had not reacted to views or visual stimulation which has been made possible by technological advances which allow humans all sorts of different views of the world. I am happy to mention that two of the artists about whom I have been most interested in the recent past, Diebenkorn and Turrell, both claim the visual experience of flying as a direct influence to their work. Diebenkorn vividly remembered his first plane flight, from New Mexico to the Bay Area (I believe) and the evidence can be seen in some of the later Berkley abstractions (50's before he switched to figuration) like the yellow one with black lines which Katherine copied. Turrell openly claims that his job as a small airplane pilot which has given him frequent views of the split heaven and earth is a direct influence to his pieces. He also mentions the multiple colors of the changing sunlight at twilight. He flew up and down the flat—and I think this is important—regions of Eastern California & the West to search for Roden Crater—he seeks to make a sculpture which directly confronts the viewer with a feeling of ½ heavens ½ earth.

> *[Diagram, top of p. 88]:* Cross-section of `Crater ->` bowl with a tiny standing viewer at the bottom and dotted sightlines forming a huge circle with the caption: `optical effect the sky "curves" creates a sphere w/ the earth. new horizon`.

I think it may be important that Diebenkorn doesn't admit to being openly influenced by anything `[above crossed-out: I thought of Diebenkorn's abstractions which critics have connected to what]`. He even would attack paintings, and "ruin" them to try to remove elements of figuration or representation which had "appeared" in them. Eventually, he stopped ruining pictures like that and would leave these elements as in *"Motorcycle Crash"* and *"The Archer"*. Diebenkorn doesn't want to think consciously about what goes into his abstractions and why. It is the critics who see evidences of the Ocean Park Architecture and landscape in the Ocean Park Series. I don't know if Diebenkorn is being completely honest about not thinking but I don't think one can completely eliminate the evidence of previous visual stimulae, a painter "names" visually even if he does not "name" things verbally. `[p. 40 *] [see the Wilson quote about memory & stars]`

Flying the other day, I was thinking about how flat the landscape really is, human beings can't do anything yet to affect this phenomena (word choice?—I don't have a definite definition in mind, though I know a thorough definition would be several pages long.) This flatness is similar to a painting's flatness—there is only so thick a painting can get—Frank Stella's (3ft?), Anselm Keiffer (1.5ft?) and likewise for the landscape, the Himalayan mountain range is the largest rupture in this flatness, 28,000m = 28km how does this compare to 40,000 km (diameter or circumference) of the earth. The earth is flatter than the paintings I have mentioned. Thus the earth's surface as seen from the sky could be the perfect subject matter for an abstract painting and the obvious reason why someone like Diebenkorn, who is somehow more true to his own visual experience than other painters who have painted abstractly, comes to make paintings reminiscent of such a landscape, which he admits having been excited by at the time. He cannot shake himself from the visual baggage which he is carrying and representing it. He is representing an image which exists only in his head, but I believe he is representing it & it does exist. The same sort of thing happens w/ Rothko over Still or Newman. People claim to see the sunset or the horizon or the sky in his works. He aims at a specific visual effect, his paint colors & tones are so carefully balanced. When he achieves the effect he desires that could very well be when the painting has matched an image he has, no matter how vague, of a large visual effect like a sunset, or the horizon.

The shadows would multiply & get complicated in the crossings of slated planes, it could become dense enough that it would be impossible to see through, or completely dark in the back from a lighted front.

**Notes on Semiotics: Bateson, *Theory of Play & Fantasy***

##### Summary of Bateson Excerpts
- Contrasts involuntary animal mood-signs (sexual odor) with human cosmetics and perfumes worn voluntarily, which nevertheless produce automatic/auto-suggestive effects even on the wearer; links this evolutionary threshold—eating of the Tree of Knowledge to discover that *signals are signals*—to human language, empathy, identification, and multiple levels of abstraction.
- Outlines **metalinguistic** messages (about language, e.g. `"the word 'cat' has no fur and cannot scratch"`) vs. **metacommunicative** messages (about the speakers' relationship, e.g. `"This is play"`, mostly implicit), where `"This is play"` expands to the paradox that these actions do not denote what they would denote.
- Notes that inside a dream, the dreamer lacks the concept `"untrue"` and cannot normally frame the dream, while schizophrenic `"word salad"` omits the `"as if"` frame-setting message, acting out metaphors literally.

## [[1994-07-31_Mortality-Andre-Diard-and-Tignes|Mortality, André Diard & Summers in Tignes]]
*1994-07-31 · scan [[attachments/Screenshot_2026-11-18_at_10.26.11_AM.png|Screenshot_2026-11-18_at_10.26.11_AM]]*

- This journal and any art possibly can be seen as a fight against death. I want to maximize my time before death—do the most w/ my life & thoughts, & possibly leave something worthwhile behind.

**7/31/94**  
André Diard died this winter. He was a key figure in the town of Tignes and a fixed memory since he ran the rental of our apartment at Les Chaudes Almes. I will always remember being on the American team golfing into the inflatable baby pools anchored off the dock of Le Terrachu *(name of the almost lost dialect of the region)*. His ringing voice (and laugh), his sharply pointed eyebrows & permanent dark tan will never be completely gone.

I remember how sad we were when the Diards gave up running Le Terrachu. All the wonderful nights we had spent there (and delicious dinners we had been served) would be a memory. Our summers were never quite the same, but we would still see the Diards now and then and Mr. Diard would ring out w/ *"Salut les Américains"* and always have a chum(b)ly relationship w/ Richie.

As we were coming up the stairs just now I mentioned my plane flight last summer w/ Laurent's Dad (Mr. Pèremezelle(?)). *"I never went up w/ André,"* Mom said—Mr. Diard took passengers for sight-tours in his small 4-seater. *"That is something I will always regret..."*

## [[1994-07-31_Unedited-Hiking-Video-and-Butors-Chien-Roi|Unedited Hiking Video Sculpture & Butor's Chien Roi (Page 91)]]
*1994-07-31 · notebook p. 91 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p026.jpg|p. 26]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_2_p027.jpg|p. 27]]*

As I was hiking up to make my first mountain painting (which needs help, but is a better start than I expected) I came up w/ another sculpture idea.

An unedited video tape of my stream of conscience while hiking — the breathing, the footsteps. The stream of thoughts that flew through my head as I actually pretended to be doing this was amazing. First (though I didn't think of it first) I would show the film in a special space which would be set up in such a way that the viewer would feel like they were in my body. Paint large black (weight) heavy shape behind — my backpack. People would stream in & out, but who would stay? I love the recap felt when viewing ski training films, what order people went, who fell when, what inadvertent things did the instructor say when filming? — I was thinking of borrowing Authier's camera, would he let me? could I rent one?

I want the tape to be uncut — a performance by me even slips out of natural feeling would be recorded & thus be natural. I would have to include comments about what I was doing, how it is hard to walk w/ camera on my eye. Start looking into a mirror? or take photo of me w/ camera? No better to let viewer form own; then the viewer will probably identify w/ it more. Would I be as brilliant as I was today? would I get self-conscious even w/ the camera? — Probably. I want to try to do this — here.

I made a connection to the new critics not wanting to quote or paraphrase, thus I would not edit, yet this small amount of time in comparison to my life is nothing more than a quote of my thoughts during that moment, thus I wouldn't try to fill in a viewer as to what had been going on in my head before. If they had questions they would have to ask me (I would be present at the showing — where I was thinking in the center of the room to get the full effect? or not present? or non-chalant mingling?) Basically, I guess I would explain as much as I do in here in this journal. What do I explain? Is there a pattern? Someone else would have to find that out, or me in a couple years.

##### Cubism Summary & Acetate Transparency Flip-Book (p. 103)
- Summarizes Arnheim (pp. 132–133) arguing against the notion that Cubism expects viewers to fly mentally around an object from multiple angles—instead, clashing aspects exist simultaneously on a flat plane.

There must be many ways of enducing the viewer's mind to form 3D mental precept — which is his mind's normal way of viewing the world. I always think of Mary (librarian at work) sitting at the other side of the circular lunch table *[Sketch of circular table and figure]* — that large flat plane took up the entire bottom half of my vision / I was very aware of the space between me & her.

Yesterday I sat on the very flat lawn and was looking at the space between Julien and Katrine's bodies, I was very aware of that distance, more so than would be possible by a drawing which included the old couple (smaller than their heads) *[Sketch of Julien & Katrine in foreground with tiny figures behind]*. Childe Hassam's ocean painting at the YUAG doesn't invoke distance as much as it brings attention to the texture which make the waves — the brightest lights are made by reflections on the surface texture of the paint. One would have to check this by moving the light or the painting around.

##### Flashlight Dark Room & Mirror-House Space (pp. 103–103v)
I thought of a very dark space where the viewer would have to bring his own light — carry a flashlight. He could investigate textures, spaces by his own ray of light, the textures their own mean. — move light source = composite images. Create single illusions w/ "place light here signs" — but he travels w/ his light to each individual viewing spot.

I thought of my transparent mirror sculpture w/ a hole underneath for the viewer's flashlight — force him to investigate on his own.
- could create effects w/ mirrors, his light bouncing off a mirror — like walking in the mirror house at Nat'l Gallery of Art, viewer had to move into space to sound the difference btw. mirrored bed & mirror chair on mirror floor.

I am controlling his viewing & making him see, he does not see till he shines his light on an object. —

## [[1994-08-12_Mont-Pourri-Climb-and-Flight-Over-Mont-Blanc|On the 4,000-Meter Crest & Flight Over Mont Blanc]]
*1994-08-12 · notebook p. 69 · scan [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p115.jpg|p. 115]], [[attachments/pages/scan_1994_1_p116.jpg|p. 116]]*

The climb was excruciating. Our guide was determined to get us, his group, up the mountain before another *cordée* that had started at about the same time. I was walking with hot dogs [crampons?]. I was so tired. The last stretch was along a knife-like crest of ice; we felt the wind for the first time, ready to blow us off. Beyond my fatigue, I was conscious only of the incredible distance below on either side of our trail of footsteps.

The top was one of the most incredible memories I have. The summit was no bigger than a queen-sized bed. We could not see any of the mountain we had just climbed, but could see for miles in every direction. This was the highest altitude I had ever been—we were just below the ceiling of 4,000 meters, my dad said. Another 50 meters and we would be over. I remember wanting to reach up or jump to get just those couple meters higher. The ceiling was right there above us.

Last summer I had another incredible experience: Laurent invited me and Julien to go on a flight with his dad. He gave us a tour of the Massif du Mont Blanc. From the air, you can see the folds in the layers of earth that make up the land we live on. The mountains and their valleys are tears through this terrain where its history of evolution can be clearly seen. Being inside that tiny plane, tossed by every turbulence caused by the mountains—irritations in the smooth passage of the layers of wind—one of the most exciting moments for me was recognizing the famous climbs which I had studied in the climbing guides: Grand Dru, Petit Dru, Dent du Géant. There is something magical about looking at these vertical walls and imagining the climber's path.
