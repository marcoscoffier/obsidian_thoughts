# Terre des Hommes and the Body

1/29 <u>Terre des hommes</u> -
Saint-Ex is always talking about his <u>death</u> - or the danger of piloting an airplane. And he died young. He says in <u>Vol de Nuit</u> that to really discover the town's secrets, he would have to "renoncer à l'action".
→ Death of the artist theme - Rimbaud, Shelley
↳ early
Saint Exupéry & Merleau Ponty → though he was not terribly young.
I am fascinated by the dramatic changes in spacial perceptions. From the tiny fly at the beginning of the 1st story in <u>Exil et Royaume</u> - to the ~~[?]~~ far horizon she studies.
Ponty's Phenomenology takes into account the two extremes of the perceptual. The body and the distant world. At the same time that the 20th c. expanded our conception of space w/ air travel, and much faster travel in general - a smaller planet or access to ~~a~~ much larger parts of it, we have become much more conscious (or literature and the arts have become much more conscious) of the minute reality of human existence. The fact that anything we think or percieve takes place w/in our body - from which we can never escape. These tiniest events of human existence take central importance in the works of Camus & Saint Exupéry:
"j'avalai ma salive et me hasardai à lui demander enfin si son vol avait été dur." - p.12 <u>Terre des Hommes</u>
The involuntary responce of the novice's nervousness, allows us to feel his situation, crawl into his body at that moment. We have all swallowed nervously. When we are lead to recall this unconscious act by the narrator we feel what he felt by reproducing the physical sensation he described in our own bodies.
