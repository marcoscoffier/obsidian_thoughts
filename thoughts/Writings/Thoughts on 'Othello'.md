> Thoughts on "Othello"  

I first met Othello at the opera, and that makes perfect sense. Othello can be an opera and should be an opera. The characters are simple and larger than life. The plot is simple and takes a long while to unfold. The end of every scene leaves you hungry for the next.

King Lear could not be an opera. There are too many characters too many questions. Wait, is this Edgar speaking or Poor Tom? Whose the bastard? Regan and Goneril? Who is mad? The fool? The king society? Too much of the play's meaning falls between the words, or hangs on the fact that a word can have two meanings. The dialogue is sparce, skeletal, and often fragmented as the disjointed thoughts of a madman. Othello's dialogue has been fleshed out. Just looking at the text shows huge blocks of carefully crafted dialogue. The rhyme scheme is so evident that it seems sing-songy at times. A song. The passages flow into one another in carefully measured meter. The developing plot draws out our emotions and interest, controlling the ebb and flow of our emotions until the cathartic climax.

Very Cheesey!
