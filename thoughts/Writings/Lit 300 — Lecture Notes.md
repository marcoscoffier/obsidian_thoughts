THEORY:

No one presides over theory-- and there are no naive questions about theory. Theory at its most radical is always naive.

Jargon is the most frequently employed tool to fight hegemony. Resistance is the basic nature of theory.

Chronology is not equal to a history of ideas. There is a distinction between a clock and the meaning of time. Chronology is necessary because there is a need for a fiction of continuity--not in itself ineluctable. Chronology is a fiction.

1/24 cont. Sir Philip Sydney:

Felt himself historically fated to perform the task of defending Literature without the patent open appeal to theology. Wanted to choose a different vector of secularization.

HE wanted to bring literary discourse into the sphere not only of what is permitted, but of what is honored with out and appeal to religious authority.

Sir Philip is important for two reasons. 1) wrote poetry of such force that it initiated the importance of poetry for the English speaking world (w/ Spencer). His novel Arcadia is a necessary movement in the development of the English novel. 2)Thinker. His Apologia is modeled on the speech of a classical lawyer. IT is a model for such a speech. He felt himself in the position of an accused. Gassen hated poetry for the same reasons as Plato and his "School of Abuse" (1579) stated that poetry was responsible for the corruption of the young and that poetry created unhealthy feelings because they are divorced even from material reality which is already a step away from contemplating God. The Apology was written to stand over Gassen's attack. Sydney re-inverts the argument in Plato. Plato is himself a poet who is guilty of imagination. Sydney was not the first to make this argument. Others have used it as privilege to Plato's superiority. He has to get people to feel in order to get them to think. The skin of poetry attracts people to the truth--this is the case for all who propagandize.

What is important about literature is that it is useful because it is beautiful. The uselessness of a semiotic aim in poetry guarantees its ability to have an emotional effect on the reader and thus open portals of thought.

Schiller 2/2
--had a life before and after Kant 1759-1804. Life spanded the French revolution and the writings of Rousseau.

--HE was obsessed by the tyranny of material science.

--His first writings were an attempt to understand how humanity which was capable of great spiriuality and beauty could be stuck so inescapably in the flesh and constant warring. Paradox of material and spiritual, between sensation and intellectual base. SPIRIT (resounding word) vs. corpulence.

The romantic revolution occured when thinkers in thinking through Kant seeked to mediate between the Kantian categories (it is funny that though he started the romanitic revolution, there is no one less romantic than Kant).

--New significance of history in Schiller. Kant had only contempt for human history, it was a mindless tale of stupid violence. History, however, is key to Schiller's achievment, it is the key to understanding who we are. Two of his earliest works were on history: 1) On the history of the Dutch rebellion against the Spanish, and 2) on the 30 yrs. War, both liberation movements.

He believed that the bad aspects of humanity were overcome in time, that they were not transcendental, but changed in time, with history-->Liberation Theory. He arranged history in his plays so that history could be reorganized and read off as a lesson-->unlocked and repatterned.

--His movemet became potent when he first encountered Kant's philosophy. Schiller wrote aseries of meditations on aesthetics on a way to gain freedom from necessity. Aesthetics liberate us from necessity....

Play-- third primary category to Kant's split level: Body/Mind Out of Kant's peculiar paradox that beauty is purposive without having purpose. A distance is needed to make this paradox make sense. A distance between the self and immediate natural needs (the painting of an apple cannot relieve hunger, nor can a beautiful aplle feed better than an ugly one). An aesthetic distance to a place of judgement makes play possible--play with the world. Paly could not be more seious in Schiller. IT is the reason why everything happens--aesthetic distance would not be available without play which mediates both mind and world.

Any people caught up in the process of civilization...must fall away from Nature by the abuse of Reason before they can return to her by use of reason.

Natural humanity of the Greeks: they were wedded to all the delights of art and all the dignity of wisdom without however falling prey to their seduciton [seduction of wisdom?]--> sense and intellect were not separate among the Greeks [as they have been separated by Kant] Why was the individual Greek qualified to be representative fo his age, and why can no single Modern venture as much? Because it was from all-unifying Nature that the former and from the all-dividing Intellect that the latter recieved their respective forms.
