# Letter to Aden and Marjorie Meinel on Atmospheric Refraction

> [!note] About this file
> About their book Sunsets, Twilights, and Evening Skies (1983). Undated.

Dear aden and Marjorie Meinel,

I have just started you r book Sunsets, twilights, and evening skies which I am thoroughly enjoying, but it one small matter just doesn't seem right to me. Mind you I am no scientist so I may very well be overlooking something,
In your figure 2.6 which depicts the geometry of atmospheric refraction two observers are represented, one on the ground and one in a plane. You have included a note which states: note that the double passage of the solar ray through the atmosphere doubles the refraction angle for the observer aloft. But, you have placed the observer aloft to the right of the observer on the ground, underneath the ground observer's imaginary extended horizon. It seems to me that you are confusing these two frames of referance: the observer in the plane should be placed above the observer on the ground, in which case the observer alof t would have his own imaginary horizon line at a certain distance above that of the observer on the ground. The sun would appear a proportionnate distance below this new horizon line (the line which belongs to the observer aloft). The observer aloft cannot be thought of as seeing with the other observer's horizon line. The double refraction of the sun is a mythical creation which has arisen because of a faulty confusion of these two different perspectives.

I know this is a small matter, but sometimes those little details just stick in one's side. I felt compelled to try and clear it up for myself. If I am wrong I would appreciate a responce. I would like to add that I have throroughly enjoyed your book.
