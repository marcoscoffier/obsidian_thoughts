# Sculpture Texts

> [!note] About this file
> The texts gathered for the installations: Stevens's 'An Ordinary Evening in New Haven' IX, the Sky Color directions, the sky-shadow lines and Hardy on twilight.

An Ordinary Evening in New Haven
--Wallace Stevens

IX

We keep coming back and coming back
To the real: to the hotel instead of the hymns
That fall upon it out of the wind. We seek

The poem of pure reality, untouched
By trope or deviation, straight to the word,
Straight to the transfixing object, to the object

At the exactest point at which it is itself,
Transfixing by being purely what it is,
A view of New Haven, say, through the certain eye,

The eye made clear of uncertainty, with the sight
Of simple seeing, without reflection. We seek
Nothing beyond reality. Within it,

Everything, the spirit's alchemicana
included, the spirit that goes roundabout
And through included, not merely the visible,

The solid, but the movable, the moment,
The coming on of feasts and the habits of saints,
The pattern of the heavens and the high, night air.

Fleeting Purple--sketch for Senior Project

Feb. 15, 1995.

Materials:
--Shadow from strong sunlight on the snow
--Orange mesh site-fence partly in the full sunlight, partly in the
shade through which the shadow can be seen.

Directions to site (frame):

Starting on the corner of Hillhouse and Trumbull walk up Hillhouse towards Kline Biology Tower and Science Hill, following the left hand sidewalk. The fence in question is the second orange mesh fence you will see. It is much longer than the first and leads down a walkway on the left side of the new Henry Luce building. Optimum viewing distance is about twenty feet away from the fence while still on the Hillhouse sidewalk, there will be a high chain link fence immediately on your left. The left hand portion of the fence is in the shade of the building on your left, the right hand side of the fence is in full sunlight. Notice how the shadow on the snow through the part of the fence which is in the shade looks to be its normal blue, whereas the shadow seen through the fence in full sunlight looks purple.

The piece is on view any sunny middle to late afternoon when there is snow on the ground and as long as the fence remains in place.

Sun Shadow
Will be on view every evening (weather permitting)
from 6:30-8:30
May 1- May 8
To get to the installation:

Procede up Prospect street to the top of science hill, which you will find is a favorite path for many student joggers. The right hand side walk is lined by an elbow-high stone wall which ends a few hundred feet from the top of the hill. A square green sign on a light post advertizing "Science Park" with an arrow tells you that you have arrived at Edwards Street. The hill flattens out considerably and on the other side of the street you see some scraggly bushes with yellow flowers intersperced with short long-needled evergreens. This corner often smells very strongly of bark.

At the first break in a low wooden fence which encloses these bushes, you see four steps which lead to a path between two much taller evergreens with redish trunks. A faded blue sign on an awkward rusted post tells you that you are entering "The William Whitman Farnum Memorial Garden given in 1931 by his widow etc." You can see many wonderfully particular trees. Each seems to have its own personality which is dampened only by the stubs of branches which have been cut because they lay too close to the ground.

As you procede up the asphalt path, it takes a turn back to the right past a tree whose lower trunk is particularly pained by wart-like protrusions which resemble sagging flesh. On your left are two trees whose downwards falling tortured branches seem awfully haggish. The sun is setting almost directly behind you. You can follow the path further or cut across the grass on your way to the stand made of rather blond wood which is the final stop on your trip.

The sun's light
at sunset cranks
around the protrusion of a hilltop
like a lever.

Its shadow
widens on one side of the pivot
as it, on the other, is flattened
to a thin line.

You are here

In the twilight of the morning, light seems active, darkness passive; in the twilight of the evening, it is the darkness which is active and crescent and the light which is in drowsy reverse.

-Thomas Hardy
