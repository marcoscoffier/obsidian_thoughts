# Cleanth Brooks: 'The Language of Paradox'

> [!note] About this file
> Notes for a section discussion. The course is a guess: Lit 300 read the New Critics.

> [!quote]- Cover sheet
> Cleanth Brooks: " The Language of Paradox"  

The theory in this essay is not particularly complicated. If there is complicaiton in this essay it is in the close readings. I'm not sure that we in this section want to discuss the details of these readings. I am no expert on Wordsworth Colderidge or Donne, and I don't think that this class is organised in the way the new critics developed. Meanign that this class hasn't been a negotiation about the interpretation of a poem. So looking at the theory in this essay is not actually very time consuming. Cleanth Brooks begins his essay by stating a prejudice of his time about the conception of paradox. He says:

Our prejudices force us to regard paradox as intellectual rather than emotional, clever rather than profound, rational rather than divinely irrrational.

I wonder if this prejudice has changed since he wrote the essay. We were told in class how large the impact of the new critics has make on the formation of discourse about literature. I wonder if this impact has changed what Brooks stated as a prejudice at the time he wrote this essay. He continues, still on the first page to say:

Yet there is a sense in which paradox is the language appropriate and inevitable to poetry. It is the scientist whose truth requires a language purged of every trace of paradox; apparently the truth which the poet utters can be approached only in terms of paradox.(p.1) Continues to say that a typical Wordsworth poem is based upon a paradoxical situation. I don't know if we should spend time on the individual closereadings. it is rather hard not to. The new critics were all about close reading.

Then we are presented a situation like that mentioned in class. Brooks is concerned with accounting for the goodness of Wordsworth's "composed upon westminster bridge. We were told that he sought to rate poems by their complexity. The language of paradox is precisely that-- alevel of complexity on which a poem operates
I am interested rather in our seeing that the paradoxes spring from the very nature of the poet's language: it is a language in which the connotations play as great a part as the denotations. And I do not mean that the connotations are important as supplying some sort of frill or trimming, somthing eternal to the real matter in hand. I mean that the poet does not use notation at all--as the scientist may be properly said to do so. The poet, within limits, has to make up language as he goes. (p.9)

The terms are continually modifying each other and thus violating their dictionary meanings(p.9)

I submit that the only way by which the poet could say what the canonization says is by paradox. More direct methodsa could be tempting but all of them enfeeble and distort what is to be said. (p.17)
