# Problem Set: Humans and Apes

> [!quote]- Cover sheet
> Marco Scoffier  
> Stephen Anderson  
> Language and Mind  
> Problem Set:Humans and Apes  

The major difference between how humans and primates learn language or the one which seemed to stand out the most to me, was mentioned in Pinker's chapter in the unedited transcripts of the primate's signing. The signs were repeated over and over in changing order, never producing sentences as human beings know them. Perhaps a small child will repeat a word from boredom or annoyance, as my younger brother would chant "chauuuu" as he rocked back and forth in his crib when he wanted to get picked up from his nap. But a child would never utter a string of words like the ape's when they were trying to be understood. All the other differences, slower learning curve, need more attentive teaching, smaller vocabulary can be attributed to physical and mental differences between us and the primates, they do not speak naturally. But the primate's weird repeated sentences are most representative of a fundamental difference in human and primate language acquisition. We conceive of language differently. Even the two or three year old child realizes something essential about language, that words are put together to form meanings that are dependent on the order in which the words are put together. You would never hear a child utter a phrase like that quoted in Pinker:

Give orange me give eat orange me eat orange give me eat orange give me you.

Almost every combination of the words was used, which can allow us to believe that the chimp has no concept that the order of the words matters at all. One can certainly understand that the chimps wants to eat an orange that you have--all the words are there and the situation can fill in the rest. The extent of this communication is not really any greater than I can have with my dog. When he wants to eat I know it, he gives a certain look. When he wants to go for a walk. He runs in circles and puts his tail between his legs. I am sure that the primate can communicate more than my dog (but one remembers Pinker's quote that his aunt claims her cat can talk). But I have to maintain that the primate's language even though he or she has learned to use signs is closer to my dog's than my own. I have to admit that I am quite convinced of Pinker and Jackendoff's claim that there is a language instinct, which allows for the remarkable fact of our human language.
