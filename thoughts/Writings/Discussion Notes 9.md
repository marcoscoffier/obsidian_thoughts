# Discussion Notes #9

> [!quote]- Cover sheet
> Marco Scoffier  
> Stephen Anderson  
> Language and Mind  
> March 30, 1995  
> Discussion notes  

I wonder why these texts always speak of rules of Universal grammar but never state them, they are always too complicated. Of course I realise that the rules haven't been decided but I think it would be interesting to know what kinds of rules the linguists are thinking about. When they say that they tried to teach Christopher an invented language which violated some of the rules of universal grammar, what were those rules it violated? I guess no one wants to be put on the record, espeacially in the layman's accounts of linguistics which we are reading, because suppositions mentioned in that environment quickly become food for a public which will think itself informed thoguh their information is faulty. Perhaps the rules are very complicated. It is funny that the most basic rules about language which are inherent to every one, could be when a scientist tries to enumerate them, very complicated, or seem to be such a tiny point that they do not grab the lay man with their importance. In any case, I am still curious about what linguists think are some possibilities for rules of universal grammar. I know someone as formulated hypotheses about them.
