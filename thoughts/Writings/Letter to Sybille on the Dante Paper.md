# Letter to Sybille on the Dante Paper

> [!note] About this file
> A cover note for a Dante paper that is not in the folder. The course and year are guessed from Sybille Reichert's other papers (spring 1993).

Dear Sybille,

I had a lot of fun writing this paper. There are some parts which are still problematic. I progressively and consciously leave a strict following of Dante. I even leave the figure of a guide, Dan T., because I wanted to seperate the pilgrim from the poet. I think that the lines which ask strength of the ancient poets need to be incorporated better wiht the rest of the text. I struggled throughout, this paper trying to follow Dante's lead and make his ideas my own and modernize them. I had trouble making my language sound modern when I was following Dante most closely. I quoted Dante the mostin the speech of Dan T. which seemed appropriate because I wanted his "sin" to be hanging on to religious notions which I do not associate with my paradise. I have also deliberately tried not to force a strict new system on to my world the way Dante did: I did initially go to New York to escape mental stagnation and visit the art museums. I considered everything in this paper very carefully, even using the word, random, at the end, which I see as a modern notion as opposed to the alternative of fate alive in Dante's time. I deliberately switched to the present tense in the epilogue. I wanted to separate it from the begining because I do not see myself talking the way I did in the begining of the canto to my girlfriend. I originally wanted to elaborate on my idea of paradise, separating it form a particular place and time: the void where the greats are, Beethoven, MIchelangelo, the creators of beauty. But, I could never get this to congeal, and since this paper was on the Inferno anyways decided to leave the boarders hazy. Last point: I tried to answer all the unanswered questions in the epilogue, implying that I have no Virgil to answer my questions, I have to come up with the answers myself. I hope you enjoy it.

Hell on Earth

Marco Scoffier
Sybille Reichert
English 129-13
Yale University
March 1, 1993
