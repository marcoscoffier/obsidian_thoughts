# Discussion Notes #2

> [!quote]- Cover sheet
> Marco Scoffier  
> Prof Stephen Anderson  
> Language and Mind  
> Jan 24, 1995  
> Discussion notes #2  

I have to say that I am getting a little bit annoyed at the mathematical proofs against how language works. That language operates as a word chain, or a system of probability seemed so wrong to me from the beginning that Pinker's extensive proof with the either/or and if/then phrases was redundant not to mention not very convincing. The mathematical disclaimer that it would be impossible to memorise even a part of all the possible sentances in the English language almost doesn't seem necessary. He tells us from the beginning what we already inherently felt, that this is the wrong way to think about langage, and then goes on to belabor this point as though we were pig-headed skeptics. I did find it qutie fascinating that geneticists use linguistic terms like "punctuation" and "letters" to discuss DNA. The similarities between these two systems could be quite fruitful to our discussion of language and mind. I would be quite interested in finding out more about the differences between a discrete combinatorial system and a blending system. Isn't language a blending system of sorts? The combination of two words is often quite different from each word alone and the position of a word in a sentance can limit its possible meanings. I suppose that language is a blending system when we look at etymology at how pieces of a word have different meanings which are blended in the new meaning of the compound word. Few people think that philosophy means love of wisdom every time they use the word, rather it has come to have its own meaning. Today one would more readily think Kant, born more than seventeen hundred years after the two parts were combined into the word to make a larger concept.
