# Discussion Notes #10

> [!quote]- Cover sheet
> Marco Scoffier  
> Prof. Stephen Anderson  
> Language and Mind  
> Discussion notes  

This chapter about the mind has left me with many questions. It seems that there are still many questions in the field. I am curious about left handed people. I don't think it was ever cleared up whether their brains have switched sides for most functions or if they are prone to certain similarites in performance.

Also I read a report of a new study that had found the largest documented difference in men's and women's brains: that women process language in both halves of the brain, and men only on one. Pinker's book seems to have been written beofre these findings. Pinker writes of the difficulties of finding the specific places in the brain where certain functions take place. I was wondering: it may be proven that very specific parts of the brain are responcible for certain tasks, but has it ever been proven that these specific places are the same in every one's brain. Couldn't the brain be a tapioca of possibilities, with all sorts of possible specific functions in specific parts of the brain, but the functions only become engraved or tied to a certain part of the brain as they are used, so as people's brains develop differently, different functions get placed in different parts of the brain. Some one who learns to speak late would have speach in a different place than someone who learned very early. This could account for the differences in people's performance over all. Someone who has a particular variety of functions in close proximity to each other will act differently than someone whose skill are mapped on the brain differently.
