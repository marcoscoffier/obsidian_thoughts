# On the Word 'Artist'

I think that the meaning of the word " artist" should be narrowed. An artist is not a writer or a painter or a sculptor or a photographer or a singer or an actor.

A good painter makes good paintings,
A good sculptor makes good scultures or objects,
A good photographer makes good pictures,
An artist makes art
which is not necessarily one of the above, those good products are not all art. I guess that in another time people talked of masterpieces. What used to be called masterpieces, are what I want to call art, except that I wish to add one more stipulation: that everyone has their own list of masterpieces, or art. In my very short time of awareness of art, have been in the presence of art a rare few times: in front of a Diebenkorn and a Sargent, and possibly a Rothko. I have never met an "artist." I have more often felt the activity of an artist. I feel it in Pollock, in Twombly, in Duchamp and in Cage, but I have never seen or experienced a work of thiers which I felt was art.#

I would like to concider being an artist to be a goal which can never be reached, that one can, at best, aspire to be an artist. As I concieve it, it is the most fleeting quality. Its is a quality shared between two people. Someone is an artist when they have captured and communicated.

But you ask, what of the person who makes the conscious decision to be an artist-- to devote their life to making art? Doesn't the decision bring attention to that person?-- that others should accept art to come from them? I don't believe that this changes our discussion at all. Some one does not decide to be an artist, but rather a painter or a sculptor or a poet. There is no magic in the decision to be a painter. As I see it, it is quite unglorious. Anyone who is in one of those professions to whose members one is used to employing the label artist, who is not an artist is really nothing. I know there are those who think that making beautiful objects or ornaments really is important and worthwhile because these small things make life more pleasant. I find life extra-ordinary to begin with, and find no interest in those ornaments. The most beautiful household objects to me are those which are most usefull. The whisk's coils are beautiful in the way they are wrapped to pass through the egg like the edge of a drill-bit is beautifull to cut through wood. The different types of teeth on different knives are beautiful for the different cut they make in different things--the vessel for how it holds its contents, to be stirred or whipped. These things are beautiful for the need they fulfill.# I fell a very strong need for art, like there is a need for life, but I feel no need for paintings, or poems, or music, or sculptures. I seek these products out to saciate my need for art. These useless products are closest to art because there is no inherent need for them. The path to the artistic experience can be most direct, because it has not been burdened by any necessity.

You cannot take anyone's word that someone is an artist. It is something you have to feel yourself. You cannot simply run on the assumption that Picasso was an artist even if thousands of art critics claim him to be and there are three shelves-full of books on him in the NJ18--artist's monographs section of any art library. It is an artist's job to communicate to you that they are an artist. That is what being an artist is. If you do not understand or feel that someone reputed as an artist is, it is not your fault. You are not in the group targeted by that artist. The aspiring artist in order to complete the equation and become an artist, must communicate. The artist is an educator. Wordsworth said it, I'm sure he wasn't the first. The artist must have full control of his medium of communication, to know to whom and how he is communicating. A great painter may only be able to communicate to those who know painting, or a composer to those who know music, or a poet to those who know poetry. That great painter, or composer or poet may also be artists, to those in his field who know enough to understand what he is communicating. The artist has a targeted audience. I realize that what began as a attempt to limit the definition of artist, has ended up doing quite the opposite, but the limitation was quite necessary as a first step: not every painter is a artist, but neither does one have to be a painter to be an artist, or a writer or a photographer or a sculptor, any of those trades. The artist can be any where. He is the communicator, the conveyor. Perhaps your parents where artists at some point, or your best friend, or some stranger that pulled you aside on the street or that sat next to you on a plane. One's life is only so short. One can never know the presence of all the artist's that where, perhaps everyone has been an artist at one point. At best one can know that small circle around you and those that made you feel that presence, that something, that art, or is it more simply put, life.

---

The artist is the person who transforms how you see your everyday commonplace world, through his of her actions, or traces of those actions. Scklovsky's definition is too literal. Art is not a poem which uses unusual metaphors: reading Dostoyevsky's description of a couch looking like a tiger, will not make you think tiger everytime you see a couch. It is much closer to Ruskin's comment that "we are to look through artworks not at them," and that "art is less beautiful than the realities it depicts."

If I walk down the street, the same street I walk down every day to my house, and it seems somehow wonderful to me, then I have been touched by art-- be that emotion the left over glow of the woman I kissed the night before, the sun casting a unique hue or the renewed eyes and ears I got because of an art object I recently had contact with. You cannot separate what is going on outside from inside: those two not completely independant systems are what produce what we know as life. Art plays in the space between these two systems: it affects one's inner system through the same channels by which one gains information about the outside system--but is furthermore created and thus contains the wonder of communication. I have left this discussion open to a personified creator of the universe--otherwise how could the sun or the stars seem so wonderful to us and fall into what I have been discussing as art. Art has been created. The great creation is much more than that possible by humans and will thus always stand high above human creations. The person who aides access to the wonder of nature's creation is not so much an artist as a nature critic--creating only access to the beauty which is already there.
