> - The folder name gives the date (28.6.1958) and the place ("Iran 6 – Alam Kuh"). Alam Kuh (about 4,850 m) is the second highest peak in Iran, in the Takht-e Suleiman massif of the Alborz. The log-and-slate village, the mule caravan, the tent camp, the glacial cirques and the flocks all fit that region, but no slide says where the village is.
> - The Super 8 film 2972072-02 has on its box "C3 – Alam Kuh 28-6-58 – Piscine Téhéran". The film shows the same outing (a mountain village, a woman with a water jug, mule trains, a climbers' camp, snow peaks) and then a Tehran swimming pool. Slides and film are almost certainly from the same trip. The slides show no pool.
> - The party wears European mountain clothes (a checked shirt, a red cap); the local people wear traditional clothes. The man in a brown jacket at the camp (slides 27–29) is probably a local guide or organiser (hypothesis). Nobody is named. The last slide (37) is a group portrait of local men and boys around a metal jug; they are not named either.
> - The black felt cloak, the axe and the huge flock crossing the stream (slides 34–36) suggest a seasonal pasture high in the mountains in June.
